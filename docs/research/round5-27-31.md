# Design research r1: five aesthetics for the Branding Designer "design language board"

Evidence tags used throughout:
- [V] observed or read directly this session (live site inspected in the browser pane, or the page text fetched).
- [S] taken from a search result summary or a page I could only partly read; treat as secondary.
- [K] my own design knowledge or inference. Build from it, but do not cite it as sourced.

Existing app aesthetics checked in `js/aesthetics/*.js`: Xerox (black toner on white, 45-degree halftone, speckle/dropout filters, edge burn, taped scrap, copier band, fluoro overprint block as a second pass), Y2K Chrome (opaque multi-stop silver gradient with hard horizon, blobby glossy pills, metaball chrome, "acid-graphics posters" listed as an influence), Holo Iridescent (near-black, 1px thin-film hairline, spectral ribbon), Neo Deco (gilt 1px lines, stepped ziggurat frame, diamonds, sunburst fan, arches), Cyber HUD (chamfered neon panels, scanlines, mono readouts), Micrographics (one giant element ringed by 7-9px microtype, registration marks, crosshairs), Mixed Type (word-by-word face swaps on a strict grid), Raw Brutalist (2px ink borders, hard shadows, mono UI).

# 1. CORPORATE VANDALISM (MSCHF energy)

## 1.1 The one idea
The prank is delivered through a flawless, bored, official-looking shell. The humour is deadpan: plain UI, plain copy, and one thing that is quietly wrong. Experts never decorate the vandalism. They let the shell stay sincere and add one intrusion.

## 1.2 References (what each does well)
1. **mschf.com** [V] https://mschf.com. Inspected live. The homepage is only a numbered text list on pure black (`#000`). Type is a custom "MSCHFSansMono" at about 46px, uppercase, line-height about 0.86 of the size, colour `#84898E` (grey), turning white on hover. Tiny index numbers sit in the left margin. A "SHOP" link carries a north-east arrow, and a small yellow "!!!" chip sits at the top right. No imagery and no decoration. The list of drop names is the whole design.
2. **store.mschf.com** [V] https://store.mschf.com. Inspected live. White page, Arial. A tiny red announcement line ("free shipping on orders $250+") sits above a loose grid of cut-out product shots, each with a faint cross/registration mark at its corners. The cart icon is a clip-art shopping cart GIF.
3. **MSCHF product page (Super Normal 2)** [V] https://store.mschf.com/products/super-normal-2. Giant heavy sans title (bright yellow `~#FFD500`) overlaps the product shot at roughly 40% of the viewport height. Option labels are tiny uppercase. The button is a glossy Windows-Vista-style blue pill with a small pencil icon. The copy is deadpan, uppercase and terse ("SOLD OUT", materials lists). The same site changes visual rules per drop.
4. **MSCHF on Sotheby's** [S] https://www.sothebys.com/en/articles/talking-art-blurred-lines-with-mschf. Gabe Whaley says they put on a "tech startup hat" or "footwear brand hat" per project. They also made a sock "in the style of Costco". This confirms the corporate mimicry is deliberate (https://jacksondahl.com/dialectic/gabe-whaley).
5. **Off-White / Virgil Abloh** [V] https://blakecrosley.com/blog/design-philosophy-virgil-abloh and https://madegooddesigns.com/off-white-brand-guidelines/ (Helvetica Bold plus quotation marks is the identity; no custom face). Mechanisms: a component names itself in quotation marks ("SHOELACES" printed on the laces); hand-labelled Helvetica on the midsole; zip-tie hang tags left on; yellow safety belt labelled "INDUSTRIAL BELT"; diagonal stripes; the "3 percent" rule (keep 97% of the original, change one precisely chosen thing). Critical read: https://lrb.co.uk/blog/2021/december/figures-of-speech.
6. **Vetements DHL tee** [S] https://www.dhl.com/discover/en-global/culture/fashion/vetements-beyond-the-t-shirt and https://i-d.co/?p=165193. A logistics logo set on a luxury garment, with no alteration. DHL went along with it. The joke is the unmodified corporate mark sitting somewhere it does not belong. Highsnobiety notes repetition killed it later (https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/vetements-ss25).
7. **Balenciaga "It's Different" (2023-24)** [S] https://theimpression.com/balenciaga-its-different-2023-ad-campaign-review/. Reuses Apple's "Think Different" typeface layout with flat product shots and anti-slogans ("Probably not what you're looking for"). Earlier SS20 fake newscast with empty slogans: https://www.nssmag.com/en/fashion/20987/balenciaga-ss20-campaign-video. The 2017 wordmark was inspired by transit signage (https://madegooddesigns.com/balenciaga-brand-guidelines/, low-authority source).
8. **Experimental Jetset** [S] https://www.typeroom.eu/article/why-experimental-jetset-living-part-typographic-history. Amsterdam studio; Helvetica as a default vocabulary, not a flourish; work limited to black, white and one flat accent; exposes printing and reproduction methods. Whitney identity (2013) is a responsive "W" system. The lesson is conviction: one face, flat colour, no effects.
9. **Supreme box logo and Barbara Kruger** [S] https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O1506977/transportation-ticket-supreme and https://grapheine.com/en/magazine/barbara-kruger-supreme-who-is-hijacking-whom/. White italic Futura-style caps in a flat red rectangle (red about `#ED1C24`/Pantone 1788C per brandkit.pro, approximate). Kruger's originals: photo plus slogan set in Futura Bold Oblique or Helvetica Ultra Condensed. A label shape that signs everything.
10. **Severance / Lumon Industries** [V] https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/severance/ and [S] https://severance.wiki/typography. Title type by Teddy Blanks is a custom Helvetica-like face with slightly odd details, left-aligned under a thin horizontal rule, based on mid-century corporate identity. The in-world corporate face is Manifold Extended, with a neo-grotesque companion. Proves a mundane, official-looking type system can feel menacing without any "effects".
11. **Jenny Holzer "AKA" etching (redaction bars)** [S] https://www.nga.gov/artworks/230967-aka. Thick black bars over lines of text plus a handwritten note. **Jamie Reid, God Save the Queen** [V] https://theconversation.com/jamie-reid-the-defiant-punk-art-of-the-man-behind-the-sex-pistols-iconic-imagery-211541: tabloid censorship bars laid over the monarch's face.
12. **The Yes Men** [S] https://press.theyesmen.org/press-archive/wikipedia.html. Fake WTO and Dow websites that passed as authentic and drew real invitations. The craft is credibility first, joke second.

## 1.3 Signature mechanisms
- **Sincere shell, one intrusion.** Layout, spacing and copy look like procurement software. Exactly one element per view is wrong: a warning that is too specific, a total that is absurd, a field label that is a confession.
- **The index list as the entire interface.** MSCHF's home is a numbered uppercase mono list with grey default and white hover [V]. Numbers sit in a narrow left column at about 10-12px, the label at hero scale. Copy this: it needs no art.
- **Scale jump with no middle.** One giant word (30-45% of viewport) versus 9-11px labels. No mid-size headings.
- **Redaction bars.** [K] Solid `#000` rectangles that exactly preserve the width of the hidden word (set the bar's width equal to the measured text width, height equal to the cap-height plus about 2px), with a 1px offset so the bar looks cut rather than drawn. On hover or select, the bar can lift to reveal text (paper-white text on a black bar reads as "declassified"). Keep a few words unredacted so the sentence still parses.
- **Quotation-mark labelling (Abloh).** [V] Wrap a noun in literal straight quotes and set it in Helvetica Bold. Use on component names: "BUTTON", "NAVIGATION", "SIGN UP".
- **Receipt strip.** [K] A 280-320px wide column of mono type, dashed `- - -` rules (use `border-top: 1px dashed`), left-aligned item, right-aligned price, uppercase, a TOTAL line, a barcode made of vertical bars of random 1/2/3px widths. Jagged top/bottom edge with a CSS `mask` (zigzag via conic-gradient). The absurd item is the joke ("EXISTENTIAL DREAD x1 ..... $0.00").
- **Warning / compliance label.** [K] A flat red rectangle, white uppercase text, 1px inner white keyline, an exclamation triangle. Copy is oddly precise ("CONTAINS 14% OPINION"). Absurd legal footnotes use superscript symbols (dagger, double dagger, section sign) that point to a footer in 9px.
- **Mundane defaults left visible.** Glossy blue system-style pill buttons (MSCHF PDP [V]), clip-art cart, default underlined links, Arial. Not stylised: the lack of stylisation is the style.
- **Gradient-free flatness.** Experimental Jetset and MSCHF both use flat fills and one accent [S][V].

## 1.4 Typography
- Real-world: Helvetica/Arial class neo-grotesk (Off-White, Experimental Jetset, Lumon-adjacent), custom mono for the index (MSCHFSansMono) [V], a Futura-oblique for label-stamps (Supreme), Helvetica Ultra Condensed for Kruger-style slogans.
- Google Fonts approximations: neo-grotesk body and headlines: **Inter Tight**, **Hanken Grotesk**, **Arimo** (Arial metrics), **Public Sans**; heavy display: **Archivo** (use width axis, 800-900), **Anton** (condensed, Kruger-style slogans), **Archivo Black**; mono index/receipt: **Space Mono**, **DM Mono**, **Martian Mono**, **IBM Plex Mono**, **Courier Prime** (receipt); Futura-oblique label: **Jost** 800 italic.
- Case and tracking: index and labels uppercase, tracking 0 to +0.02em (MSCHF's own is normal [V]); huge headlines tracked tight, -0.04 to -0.06em, line-height 0.82-0.9. Footnotes 9-10px uppercase, +0.04em.
- Size ratios: headline : body : footnote about 14 : 1.4 : 1 (for example 140px / 14px / 10px). No intermediate sizes.
- Headline construction: one or two words, left-aligned, bleeding or touching the edge; or overlaid on a product/shape at 40% opacity multiply (Super Normal PDP [V]).

## 1.5 Colour and brand mapping
- Fixed (never client): black `#000`, paper white `#FFF`, and the hazard layer: warning red (about `#F00007`, per a brand-listing for mschf.xyz [S]; Supreme about `#ED1C24`) and safety yellow (about `#FFD500` [V]). The hazard layer is at most about 5% of the area.
- Mapping: **client primary = the official shell** (header bar, primary button fill, link colour), kept pristine and boring so the vandal layer reads as a hijack. **Client secondary = receipt paper tint / form fills.** **Client accent = the single "tiny official" notice** (the announcement line above the grid, the "!!!" chip). **Dark = `#000` page. Light = white page, never cream.** If the client's accent is already red or yellow, keep the warning label in the fixed hazard and make the client colour the shell only.
- Contrast rule: grey `#84898E` on black for resting list items passes about 6:1; keep that.

## 1.6 UI translation
- **Header:** a 1-line bar: client wordmark small at left, then a numbered nav in mono uppercase, "SHOP ↗" style arrow link at right, plus one yellow "!!!" or warning chip. 36-40px tall, no shadow, no border-radius.
- **Hero:** see 1.7.
- **Buttons:** (a) sincere primary: client fill, 0 radius or 2px, uppercase 12px label with mundane wording ("CONTINUE", "OK"); (b) the sabotage variant: a second button labelled "ALSO OK" or "NO" that does the same thing; (c) a glossy system pill as the single "wrong" button. States: hover swaps grey to white and underlines. Pressed = invert.
- **Tabs / segmented control:** a numbered index row ("01 TERMS 02 PRIVACY 03 OTHER TERMS") with active item white and others grey `#84898E`; the active tab gets quotation marks: "TERMS".
- **Toggles:** a native-looking checkbox/switch with deadpan label ("I ACKNOWLEDGE THAT I HAVE ACKNOWLEDGED"); on = black fill with a white check; off = 1px outline. Toggles can carry a footnote symbol.
- **Inputs:** 1px black outline, 0 radius, label above in 10px uppercase, placeholder as the joke; validation messages as official error codes ("ERR 0x00C0FFEE: LOOKS FINE").
- **Cards/panels:** a "notice" panel: 1px black border, a header strip in client primary with a 10px uppercase title, body in 14px, footnote 9px. Receipt panel for totals.
- **Dense list:** the MSCHF index: numbered rows, mono uppercase, grey to white hover, right-aligned status ("SOLD OUT", price). 12-16 rows with 0.86 line-height reads as dense but calm.
- **Progress:** a plain text counter "STEP 2 OF 3" plus a 2px black bar; or a bar that overshoots 100% and says "103%".
- **Badges:** red warning chip, yellow "!!!" chip, black "REDACTED" bar.
- **Dividers:** dashed receipt rules, a 1px black line with a tiny cross/registration mark at the ends (as on the store grid [V]).
- **Footer:** 9px legal block: uppercase, numbered footnotes, a "NO REFUNDS ON SARCASM"-type line, a cookie notice with two equal buttons. The footnotes should be the longest text on the board.
- **Motion (static-safe):** only colour/underline changes and instant swaps. No easing flourishes. A redaction bar can slide aside in 120ms linear.

## 1.8 Signature hero art (SVG/CSS from brand colours)
1. **The hijacked notice:** an official-looking client panel (client primary header strip, wordmark text). Over it: a giant uppercase word in `Archivo` 900, a paragraph of mono fine print with three redaction bars of exact word width, a red warning label tilted 0 degrees (not rotated: rotation reads as "design"), and a small receipt column to the right with an absurd total.
2. **The index:** just the MSCHF list for the client's own content (nav items, products) as the hero. Very high impact for almost no art.
3. **Quoted object:** a flat client-colour product silhouette with a "LABEL" in quotation marks and a zip-tie tag drawn as a thin rectangle.

## 1.9 Pitfalls
- Over-styling the mess: rotation, tape, grunge. That is Xerox or Xerox Punk. Here the shell must be tidy.
- Making the joke legible only if you read every line. One intrusion per screen, not ten.
- Fake legal text that is gibberish or lorem ipsum. Deadpan needs plausible syntax.
- Using real brands' logos or real law firms; the Vetements-style trick requires licences. Parody generic corporate language instead.
- Dropping the red/yellow hazard to client colours. The recognisable alarm is what makes the shell feel hijacked.
- Rounded cards, soft shadows, gradients: the wrong register (MSCHF's flatness [V]).
- Repeating a gag until it is a template (Highsnobiety's complaint about Vetements [S]).

## 1.10 Distinctness
- **Raw Brutalist:** also hard-edged, but sincere and structural (2px ink borders, offset shadows). Corporate Vandalism has no borders as decoration, uses grey index lists, hazard red, receipts and footnotes. Brutalist says "honest"; this one says "official and lying".
- **Mixed Type / Micrographics:** microtype is similar; Vandalism uses legal prose and receipts, not registration/crosshair ornament, and it has a hazard colour layer.
- **Xerox:** photocopy texture. Vandalism is clean, born-digital.

# 2. RELIGIOUS FUTURISM (the digital cathedral)

## 2.1 The one idea
Light as the sacred material, stone as the ground, and ritual restraint as the pace. A sacred OS does not decorate itself; it uses a few monumental forms (arch, ring, rose, ground of gold or black), huge silence, and tiny liturgical annotation.

## 2.2 References
1. **Dune (2021) poster/title** [V] https://fontsinuse.com/uses/44880/dune-2021-poster. Two-colour gold and black; custom ambigram title; quote in all-caps Minion (a plain classical serif, not a fantasy face); text set on a circle, ruled borders, hexagon motifs. Lesson: the sacred read comes from two colours and a classical text face, not ornament.
2. **Dune: Part Two UI graphics by Territory Studio** [V] https://territorystudio.com/project/dune-part-two/. Holographic globes shaped like karst terrain, in a "volume of light" so projection feels physical; strict military formation versus organic Giger-like forms; an insectoid script typeface; Mentat chant vibrations as UI research. Lesson: interface as ritual instrument, physical light.
3. **Blade Runner 2049 / Wallace Corp UI** [V] https://territorystudio.com/project/blade-runner-2049/ and [S] https://www.designweek.co.uk/graphic-design-blade-runner-2049-film-dad-festival/. Wallace screens are stark black-and-white, minimal and geometric, deliberately cleaner than every other interface in the film. Others show wear (warp, ghosting, glitch). Lesson: sacred-powerful = fewer elements, perfect alignment.
4. **Foundation (Apple TV+) main titles by Imaginary Forces** [S] https://www.stashmedia.tv/apple-tv-foundation-main-titles-by-imaginary-forces and https://imaginaryforces.com/project/foundation-season-3. A flat "living" mural turned into a 3D animated dreamscape; season 3: radiant particles, shifting patterns, organic vs mathematical forms. Lesson: slow, monumental, reverent motion.
5. **Gerhard Richter, Cologne Cathedral window (2007)** [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne_Cathedral_Window. 11,263 glass squares in 72 colours (palette drawn from the cathedral's medieval/19th-century glass), placed by random number generator with mirrored lanes and a few manual corrections. This is the most important single "digital stained glass" precedent: pixel-mosaic, not figurative glass.
6. **Byzantine gold ground** [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_ground and inverse perspective https://perspectiveresearchcentre.com/reverse-inverse-perspective-images/. Gold ground erases landscape, perspective and shading and puts the figure in timeless space; inverse perspective makes parallels diverge toward the viewer. Mandorla is the almond-shaped glory around a sacred figure (https://nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/NG702).
7. **Björk: Biophilia app** [S] https://snibbe.com/biophilia and https://www.moma.org/collection/works/179295. A galaxy hub of ten bright stars; each star opens a self-contained world. Lesson: constellation navigation instead of menus.
8. **Arca: KICK series, art by Frederik Heyman** [S] https://www.creativereview.co.uk/arca-kick-album-art-video-frederik-heyman/. Computer-generated sets mixing gore and majesty, ancient symbolism and post-human futures; every cover is a still from one video. Lesson: sacred imagery is permitted to be uncomfortable.
9. **Rick Owens** [S] https://luxe.digital/brands/rick-owens/. Dark, draped, brutalist luxury in black, dust and pearl; Catholic monastic roots reported; furniture treated as monolith. Lesson: bone and obsidian as a material palette.
10. **Elden Ring / Diablo IV type and UI** [S] https://madegooddesigns.com/?p=9250 and https://www.rpgsite.net/news/9492-diablo-iv-discusses-ui-design-coop-controller-support-and-cannibals-in-first-quarterly-update. Inscription-style high-contrast serif wordmark (Elden Ring); sparse HUD. Diablo IV logo built on Exocet, pushed spiky with distress and a red glow. Lesson: where the genre gets cheap, and what restraint buys.
11. **Spirituality and design (It's Nice That)** [V] https://www.itsnicethat.com/features/spirituality-and-design-thematic-graphic-design-080223. Shun-Zhi Yang's Buddhist sutra typography with prayer-bead droplets; Saint Church Hackney replacing gold-embossed insignia with animated type; the Franciscus typeface for the Basilica of Assisi; the article critiques "Golden Ratio" as a commercial default.
12. **Italian Futurists on religious painting** [S] https://www.italianfuturism.org/manifestos/concerning-futurist-religious-painting/. Historical precedent for sacred subject plus machine-age vocabulary.

## 2.3 Signature mechanisms
- **Two-colour discipline.** Dune's poster is gold and black only [V]. Gold is a *line and glyph* colour, almost never a fill.
- **Ground choice:** obsidian (`~#0A0A0C`) with a very low-contrast stone texture, or bone (`~#EDE6D6`) for light mode. Never pure white and never pure gold-on-white.
- **Oxidised gold.** [K] A restrained, desaturated metal: `#B8964B` bright, `#8C6D2E` mid, `#5E4A1E` shadow, drawn as a vertical 3-stop gradient on lines/glyphs; add a 0.5px lighter top edge. Avoid the yellow 'casino' `#FFD700`.
- **Arch (ogive) panels.** [K] A panel shape with a pointed arch top: `clip-path: path()` or an SVG with two arcs meeting at a point; 1px gold hairline with a 4px inner inset second hairline (double rule echoing leading, but not Neo Deco's stepped ziggurat corners).
- **Halo progress.** [K] A thin concentric ring (stroke 1px) with 60 tiny tick marks, a second thicker arc (2.5px) in gold gradient for progress, and a small filled disc at the arc's head. A 6-8% outer glow in the stained-glass accent. The percent in small caps serif inside.
- **Mandorla frame.** [K] An almond (vesica piscis: two intersecting circles) around selected content; use for selected avatars/cards.
- **Digital stained glass (Richter method).** [S] A grid of square cells (for example 24x16) from a limited palette (8-12 colours derived from the brand), assigned by seeded pseudo-random with mirrored lanes; a 2px near-black "came" (lead line) grid between cells; a soft inner bloom behind (a blurred copy at 40% opacity) so it reads as transmitted light, not pigment.
- **Figurative stained glass alternative.** [K] Voronoi or rose-window radial tessellation with 3-4px black leading; each pane a luminous gradient (center lighter, edges deeper). Always back-light.
- **Tiny technical annotations as liturgy.** [K] 8-10px small caps or mono: Roman numerals (I, II, IIII), canonical-hour times ("MATINS 03:00"), coordinates, verse numbers as list indices ("§ 12:4"), set with +0.18em tracking and gold at 70% opacity. Contrast stays at 4.5:1 by using bone not gold for the small text.
- **Monumental serif.** One word, 120-220px, weight 300-400, tracking +0.04 to +0.12em for caps (Cinzel-like) or -0.02em for lowercase (Cormorant-like), line-height 0.95, centred on the axis. Centre-axis symmetry is the layout.
- **Slow ceremonial motion.** [K] 700-1200ms ease-in-out fades; halo arc sweeps once; light "enters" via an opacity+blur transition (blur 8px to 0). Static-safe: show the end state; no autoplay loops.
- **Inverse-perspective touch (optional).** [S] Panels in a stack may widen slightly with distance (a 1-2 degree outward skew) to evoke icon space. Use sparingly: it breaks alignment.

## 2.4 Typography
- Real-world: Minion caps and a custom ambigram title (Dune [V]); inscription high-contrast serif (Elden Ring [S]); Franciscus-type sacred lettering [V]; neo-grotesk companion for system text.
- Google Fonts: monumental caps: **Cinzel** (Trajan-like, regular/semi-bold), **Cinzel Decorative** (sparingly); high-contrast display: **Cormorant** / **Cormorant Garamond** (300-500), **Gloock**, **Bodoni Moda**, **Playfair Display SC**; Marcellus/Marcellus SC; **Forum**; **Italiana**; small caps body: **EB Garamond**, **Spectral SC**, **Cormorant SC**; wide technical sans for Dune-like labels: **Syncopate**, **Michroma**, **Krona One** (use with restraint; Orbitron is cliché); mono annotation: **IBM Plex Mono**, **Space Mono**, **DM Mono**; blackletter accent only: **Grenze Gotisch**, **UnifrakturCook**.
- Pairing recipe: Cinzel or Cormorant caps for headlines (tracking +0.08em), EB Garamond for reading, IBM Plex Mono 9px for annotations. Three voices, no more.
- Size ratios: hero 160-220px / section 28-36px / body 16-18px / annotation 9-10px: very wide gaps.

## 2.5 Colour and brand mapping
- Fixed: obsidian `#0A0A0C` (dark), bone `#EDE6D6` (light), oxidised gold ramp above (shift hue 5-10 degrees toward the client's warmth so it harmonises, but keep it recognisably gold), leading-black `#050505`.
- **Client primary = the principal glass / the halo glow** (luminous: lift lightness to about 55-65%, keep chroma). **Client secondary = second glass family and the arch's inner wash.** **Client accent = the one "relic" jewel** (selected state, progress head, one pane). **Dark = obsidian. Light = bone.** Stained glass is always light-on-dark, which is why the dark mode is canonical; light mode should look like daylight through glass on bone (reduce glow, thicken leading).
- Do not let gold carry the brand: it is a material.

## 2.6 UI translation
- **Header:** centred wordmark in Cinzel caps, a hairline gold rule beneath with a small diamond-less centre notch (a tiny vesica), nav in annotation-size caps flanking; a tiny canonical-hour clock.
- **Hero:** rose window or arch with halo (2.8).
- **Buttons:** primary = gold hairline outline pill-less rectangle (0 radius) with Cinzel 12px caps +0.2em tracking; hover fills with a vertical gold gradient and text turns obsidian; or primary = solid client colour glass with a 1px leading outline. Pressed = inner glow dims. Secondary = text with a gold underline of 1px at 6px offset.
- **Tabs/segmented control:** arch-topped tabs (each tab's top is a ogive); active tab has gold line and a halo dot above it. Segmented control: a row of three vesica shapes with the selected one filled with glass colour.
- **Toggles:** a vertical or horizontal "reliquary" switch: hairline gold track; the thumb is a small circle with a halo ring that fills when on; off = bone ring.
- **Inputs:** an underline field (1px gold at 40%) with a tiny cross-mark at the right end; focus = line brightens and a short glow; labels in small caps annotation.
- **Cards/panels:** arch-top panels, double hairline inset, stone grain at 3% noise; selected = mandorla outline.
- **Dense list:** a "litany": index in Roman numerals or section signs, entry in small-caps serif, right-aligned mono status; hairline gold at 15% between rows; row height 36px; hover sets a 2px glass-colour left rule that glows.
- **Progress:** halo ring (2.3) for the main one; a "rosary" strip of beads for steps (filled bead = gold, current bead = glass glow).
- **Badges:** vesica pill with annotation caps; "VIGIL", "CONSECRATED" style states.
- **Dividers:** a thin gold rule with a centre vesica or a short run of 3 dots (a rosary mark).
- **Footer:** a bone/obsidian apse: centred small caps colophon, a row of tiny index numerals, the date written as a liturgical date.
- **Motion:** slow fades; one halo sweep on load; nothing bounces.

## 2.7 Signature hero art (SVG/CSS)
1. **Halo and rose window:** a 3-ring concentric halo with ticks, 12 radial panes tinted from the client palette with black leading and a blurred backlight; a monumental serif word on the central axis.
2. **Pixel cathedral (Richter):** a tall arch-shaped window whose fill is a seeded random mosaic from the client palette, with mirrored lanes; leading lines; a beam of light projected onto obsidian floor as a skewed gradient quad.
3. **Gold-ground icon:** a flat gold-ground disc (gradient with grain), a nimbus ring, a client-colour mandorla, and one monogram glyph at the centre; inverse perspective hint via outward-skewed side panels.

## 2.8 Pitfalls
- Gold gradient fills everywhere, casino yellow, sparkle effects: the Diablo/Elden-menu clipart look (generic gold-bordered stock UI is exactly what search returns for "gold ornate" [S]).
- Fantasy fonts (Uncial, decorated blackletter headlines): reads as tabletop RPG.
- Cross/crucifix, real religious symbols of a specific faith: use neutral geometry (arch, ring, vesica, rose). Avoid anything that a client could find offensive.
- Orbitron + gold + rays: "sci-fi church" parody.
- Over-glowing: the glass should be backlit, not neon.
- Skipping silence: no space = no sanctity (Wallace UI is minimal [V]).
- Treating "golden ratio" as an excuse for design (It's Nice That critique [V]).

## 2.9 Distinctness
- **Neo Deco:** gilt hairlines and symmetry but stepped ziggurat corners, diamonds and sunburst on a Gatsby register. Religious Futurism uses ogive arches, halos, mandorla and luminous glass; gold is a thin material, not decoration; serif and small caps replace Deco geometric sans.
- **Aurora Noir / Holo Iridescent:** near-black and light effects but dev-tool/spectral. Here light is saturated stained glass with black leading, not a spectrum.
- **Editorial Serif:** quiet serif, light ground. Here: monumental, centred, ritual, dark.
- **Cyber HUD / Micrographics:** technical annotation exists, but it is liturgical (numerals, hours, verses) and serif/small caps, not crosshair/registration mono.

# 3. XEROX PUNK (the underground manifesto)

## 3.1 The one idea
A wall of pasted things. It is not a clean copy of one page; it is many sources (newsprint cut-outs, photocopies, tape, marker) stuck over each other on a dark street wall, with one fluorescent hit and the reader's own hand marking up the page.

## 3.3 What makes it different from the app's existing Xerox
| Xerox (existing) | Xerox Punk (new) |
|---|---|
| One office photocopy of one sheet | Many different sources pasted onto a wall |
| White paper ground, black toner | Dark wall ground (asphalt/black), paper scraps on top |
| Threshold + speckle + edge burn as the look | Collage and hand-intervention as the look; grain is secondary |
| Fluoro as a second-pass overprint block | One fluoro as marker/highlighter/tape/spray, used on state and emphasis |
| Heading is a coherent typeset line | Headline = ransom-note tiles: every letter has its own font, size, ground, rotation |
| Tape, tear-off strip, copier band | Wheatpaste layers (wrinkle, overlap, torn edges), safety pins, marker circles/cross-outs, censor bars |
| Neat photocopy order | Overlap and collision; elements cover each other |
| States = filled or outlined | States = circled / crossed out / scribbled over |

Keep the two apart by rule: Xerox Punk never shows a white page as the main surface, never uses a copier edge band, never uses a coarse halftone dot screen as the hero, and never composes in one coherent column.

## 3.4 Signature mechanisms
- **Ransom-note headline.** [V] Each letter is a tile cut from a different source: serif/slab/condensed/mono; its own ground (newsprint cream `#E9E3D0`, white, black, fluoro, photocopy grey); its own size within a +/-35% band of the cap height; rotation +/-2 to 6 degrees; vertical offset +/-6px; a 1px darker edge and a faint shadow. Keep 1 tile in 5 fluoro. Maximum 14 letters per line or it becomes noise. Tile widths should follow glyph widths with 4-8px padding so it still reads as a word.
- **Wheatpaste layers.** [K] Each panel is a paper sheet with jagged torn edges (SVG `feTurbulence` + `feDisplacementMap` on the clip path, scale 6-10), a faint wrinkle texture (a low-frequency noise multiplied at 8-12%), a 2-3px drop shadow offset down/right, and overlap of 8-24px with its neighbour. Wall behind is flat black with 3-5% noise and a few older torn-poster fragments in 6% opacity.
- **Cut-up black bars (censor/tabloid).** [V] Solid black bars over an eyes line or text (Reid's God Save the Queen).
- **Marker annotation layer.** [K] Rough hand-drawn SVG paths in the fluoro or white: a circle drawn as an imperfect double loop (two ellipses offset 3-5px, stroke-linecap round, stroke 3-4px, `stroke-dasharray` slightly broken), an underline, an arrow, a cross-out as two crossing strokes at unequal lengths. Selected = circled; deselected/disabled = crossed out; deleted = scribbled.
- **Pins and clips.** [V] Safety pin and bulldog clip silhouettes (Reid [V]) fastening a corner; draw a safety pin as a 2-loop SVG path with a metallic grey gradient.
- **Stamped navigation.** [K] Rubber-stamp text in a distressed box, rotated -3 degrees, ink bleed via an `feTurbulence` mask, in fluoro or bone. Not a rounded button.
- **Misregistration.** [V] Offset an overprint colour by 2-4px from its black line art.
- **Hand captions (Pettibon).** [S] Small handwritten line under an image that comments or quotes.
- **Photocopy-of-photocopy only on images.** [K] Apply grain/threshold only to photos and scraps, not to UI text, so it stays legible.

## 3.5 Typography
- Real-world: mismatched newspaper and magazine letters [V]; stencils, Letraset and hand lettering [V]; typewriter and handwriting [S]; heavy condensed grotesque for slogans (Kruger-style).
- Google Fonts: ransom tile pool: **Anton**, **Alfa Slab One**, **Abril Fatface**, **Playfair Display** 900, **Old Standard TT**, **Libre Caslon**, **Rozha One**, **Bebas Neue**, **Staatliches**, **Rubik Mono One**, **Chonburi**; typewriter/body: **Special Elite** (note: the existing Xerox already uses Special Elite and Courier Prime; prefer **Cutive Mono**, **Courier Prime** in small amounts, or **IBM Plex Mono**); stencil: **Black Ops One**, **Stardos Stencil**, **Saira Stencil One**, **Big Shoulders Stencil**; handwriting/marker: **Permanent Marker**, **Rock Salt**, **Reenie Beanie**, **Covered By Your Grace**, **Caveat Brush**; blackletter tile (rare): **UnifrakturMaguntia**, **Pirata One**; distressed: **Rubik Distressed**, **Rubik Spray Paint**.
- Rules: headline = ransom tiles (never a single face), sub-head = one stencil or heavy condensed, body = a single typewriter/mono at 13-14px with generous leading (1.6), annotations = marker script at about 1.3x body size in fluoro/white. Case: mixed per tile, but body copy sentence case.

## 3.6 Colour and brand mapping
- Fixed: wall black `#0A0A0A` and bone/newsprint `#E9E3D0` (paper scraps), plus the rule that there is exactly **one** fluoro.
- **Client primary becomes the fluoro** (push chroma up and lightness to about 70-80%; if it cannot be pushed to fluoro, replace with a Day-Glo yellow-green `#D4FF00` or hot pink `#FF2E93`, noting Reid's yellow+pink pairing [V]). **Client secondary = the second pen (marker ink, tape colour), used at under 5%.** **Client accent = a single tile in the ransom headline and the stamp.** **Dark = wall black. Light = paper (newsprint) panels, not a white page.** If the client's palette is entirely muted, the fluoro is a fixed override and the client colours go to the tape and stamp.
- Contrast: bone on black 14:1; fluoro on black high; never fluoro text on newsprint.

## 3.7 UI translation
- **Header:** a strip of black paper with the wordmark as a ransom headline (small), nav as stamped labels with one circled; a pinned corner.
- **Hero:** see 3.8.
- **Buttons:** a paper tile with a hard black 2px border and a 3px hard offset shadow, text in stencil caps; hover: tile rotates +1 degree and gets a fluoro highlighter swipe behind the text; pressed: a black X scribble crosses it briefly. Primary = fluoro tile with black text; secondary = newsprint tile.
- **Tabs:** pasted labels of different widths (not equal); selected = circled in marker; unselected = plain; disabled = crossed out.
- **Toggles:** a hand-drawn tick box: off = empty square; on = a marker X plus a fluoro fill. Or a two-label switch where the inactive word is crossed out.
- **Inputs:** a ruled line with typewriter text on a scrap, label as a marker note; focus = fluoro underline smear; error = a black bar.
- **Cards/panels:** wheatpaste sheets with torn edges, overlapped; a pin on one corner; a handwritten caption.
- **Dense list:** a pasted newsprint column: typewriter rows, ruled lines, a hand-circled item, one row censor-barred; row numbers as stamped "No." Overlap the column's edge with a torn strip.
- **Progress:** a tally mark count (five-bar gates) or a fluoro highlighter bar that wobbles; completed steps struck through.
- **Badges:** stamps and tape strips with text (labelled "NEW", "BANNED").
- **Dividers:** a torn paper edge, a strip of tape, or a marker zigzag.
- **Footer:** a pasted list of zine-style credits, an address in typewriter, "DO IT YOURSELF" style stamp, a small safety pin.
- **Motion (static-safe):** 2-frame jitter (steps(2)) on hover for rotation; marker strokes draw on (stroke-dashoffset) once. Reduced-motion = static.

## 3.8 Signature hero art
1. **The wall:** a black ground; three overlapping torn paper sheets (newsprint, photocopy grey, fluoro); a ransom headline of the client's name or tagline; a black censor bar across a photo placeholder (use a halftone-less flat silhouette); a safety pin; one circled word.
2. **The Union Jack move without the flag:** a torn rectangle of the client's primary pattern reassembled with bulldog clips and pins (an abstract of Reid's torn flag [V], no flag, no copyrighted artwork).
3. **The mark-up:** a list of pasted items where the unwanted ones are crossed out and the chosen one is circled in fluoro with a handwritten note.

## 3.9 Pitfalls
- White-paper-as-ground (turns into the existing Xerox).
- Ransom tiles that are too uniform (same font, same size): looks like a "ransom font" filter. Real ones vary height and baseline.
- Over-rotation: more than about 6 degrees looks sloppy; random rotation of every element looks AI.
- Too many fluoros: one only [V Reid used two on one cover, but pink+yellow is a pair acting as one].
- Illegible body copy (grain on text) or heavy blur.
- Fake "grunge" brushes and splatters from stock packs; generic ripped-paper PNG with a clean drop shadow.
- Using Sex Pistols/Reid artwork, the Union Jack plus Queen, Black Flag bars, Kruger layouts or the Supreme box logo directly. Describe and re-make the technique with original content.
- The "Hot Topic" look: skulls, chains, spikes.

## 3.10 Distinctness
- **Xerox:** see 3.3 table.
- **Raw Brutalist:** hard borders but ordered; Xerox Punk is chaotic and handmade.
- **Mixed Type:** per-word face swaps are on a strict grid with aligned cap heights. Ransom tiles deliberately break alignment and have grounds.
- **Riso Print:** inks and misregistration but clean printmaking; Xerox Punk is torn and pasted.

# 4. ACID GRAPHICS (the illegal warehouse rave)

## 4.1 The one idea
Vibrating colour, stretched and warped letterforms, and dense information, laid over a hidden structure. Eye on Design describes it as bright, neon-green-heavy colour with warped type, a dark sense of humour and a streak of "antidesign" [V]. The best examples have an underlying order (Saville's "gridded calm" [V]); the worst are noise.

## 4.2 References

## 4.3 Signature mechanisms
- **Vibrating colour pairs.** [V] Place two high-chroma, near-equal-luminance colours edge to edge so the border shimmers (green `#B6FF00` vs violet `#6A00FF`; orange vs blue). Do not put thin type on a vibrating edge.
- **Stretch and warp as the rule.** Headline set in an extended or ultra-wide face and further transformed: `transform: scale(1.0, 2.6)`, skew -12 degrees, or an SVG `feDisplacementMap` driven by a sine-wave `feTurbulence` (baseFrequency 0.004 0.02, scale 30-60). Letters can run off the edge. Reversed or upside-down secondary lines [V].
- **Optical patterns.** [V][K] Op-art ripple: `repeating-radial-gradient` with 8-14px alternating bands in the vibrating pair; moire by overlaying two slightly rotated line gratings; checker/grid warped by a lens; concentric "form constants" (spirals, tunnels, funnels, honeycombs, Klüver) [V].
- **Chrome as a texture, not the system.** Liquid metal type with a horizon line is one trick; the acid look can use it for one word only. The app's Y2K Chrome already owns chrome, so Acid Graphics should lead with colour vibration, warp and density.
- **Density with hidden grid.** [V] Underneath: a 12-column grid and a baseline unit; on top: 5-9 overlapping layers. Counting columns taught Banks grid systems [V].
- **Information strips.** [K] Event strip: a horizontal band of uppercase mono info (DATE / PLACE / DOORS / LINEUP), a marquee scroll (static-safe: show a fixed slice), and a sponsor/logo strip of invented marks (Designers Republic's invented sponsor logos [S]).
- **Corporate parody.** [S] Faux sponsor/team logos and product marks; low legal exposure on flyers [S]. Use invented brands only.
- **Scan-bleed distortion.** [V] Print/scan-loop letters until they bleed; emulate with `feGaussianBlur` + `feComponentTransfer` threshold at stdDeviation 1-3 on display type only.
- **Kline-type blocks.** [V] A massive block of type at one end and shards at the other; scale jumps between close-up and long shot.
- **Smiley/eyes iconography.** [V] Recurring but cliché; use a tabs-grid, eye or asterisk burst instead.
- **Stretching hover.** [K] On hover, a heading's `font-stretch`/`scaleX` animates from 100% to 150% and a vibration offset appears; static-safe: show the stretched variant as the active state.

## 4.4 Typography
- Real-world: wide or squared techno sans (Eurostile-like [S]), heavy metal/chrome lettering, bad rave-flyer fonts used on purpose [V], Letraset sheets [V], brutalist post-gothic [V].
- Google Fonts: extended/warp: **Unbounded**, **Syne** (ExtraBold), **Dela Gothic One**, **Rubik Mono One**, **Tilt Warp**, **Climate Crisis** (variable, melting), **Anybody** (width axis), **Krona One**, **Bungee**, **Monoton**, **Nabla** (colour font, 3D chrome-like), **Zen Dots**, **Michroma**/**Audiowide** (Eurostile-ish); labels/info: **Space Mono**, **Share Tech Mono**, **DM Mono**; clean body: **Space Grotesk**, **Archivo** (width axis), **Chakra Petch**.
- Size ratios: hero 200-320px (can overflow) / info strip 11-13px mono uppercase tracking +0.1em / body 14px; a third or fourth mid-size is unusual.
- Headline construction: one word stretched vertically past the container; a second line outlined (stroke 2px, fill none) and offset 6-10px; the outline line is shifted along a sine path.

## 4.5 Colour and brand mapping
- Fixed: black `#050505`, chrome silver neutrals `#C9CED6`/`#6C7480`, and the requirement that the two dominant colours vibrate against each other.
- **Client primary = the dominant acid colour** (push chroma to the maximum the hue allows, lightness 55-75%). Derive **the clash colour = primary hue +150 to +180 degrees at equal luminance**. **Client secondary = the pattern's second band or tinted shadow.** **Client accent = one spot colour** (sparkle, ticket stub, active state). **Dark = black. Light = either stark white or a single full-bleed primary field.** Acid green `#B6FF00` and ultraviolet are fixed fallbacks if the brand has no saturated colour.
- Keep a neutral rest area (black or white) around 30% so density has somewhere to breathe.

## 4.6 UI translation
- **Header:** a scrolling event strip (ticker) in mono caps over a black band; wordmark stretched and clipped by the strip; nav as stacked uppercase items with the active one inverted.
- **Hero:** see 4.7.
- **Buttons:** a stretched pill or rectangle with a vibrating border (2px inner in clash colour), uppercase extended face; hover stretches the button 6-10% horizontally and flips colours; pressed = invert with a 2px chromatic offset.
- **Tabs / segmented control:** a ticket strip: each tab is a perforated stub (a dashed 1px divider with half-circle cut-outs at the ends); active = filled acid colour with extended type.
- **Toggles:** a pill that stretches: off = narrow, on = wide with a second-colour fill; thumb is a cross, asterisk or smile-less circle.
- **Inputs:** a heavy 2px bottom rule in the clash colour, label in mono caps above, the entered text in the extended face; focus: the underline starts to ripple (an SVG waveform).
- **Cards/panels:** edge-to-edge colour blocks that overlap; one card has the op-art ripple as a background at 20% opacity; use `mix-blend-mode: difference` for overlaps on one or two elements only.
- **Dense list:** a lineup/tracklist: time/name/room in mono caps, large first column numerals, alternating vibrating-pair row backgrounds at low contrast; the headliner row in the extended face at 3x size. 14-20 rows.
- **Progress:** a stretched band that fills with vibrating stripes (a `repeating-linear-gradient` animated; static-safe: show stripes at the current fill).
- **Badges:** ticket stubs, "18+", "SOLD OUT" in invented sponsor style.
- **Dividers:** a marquee rule of repeated `* * * * *` or a wavy SVG line.
- **Footer:** dense: invented sponsor logos row, tiny disclaimer, tide of coordinates and a repeated acronym; keep it on a grid.
- **Motion:** warp on hover, slow marquee, ripple; reduced motion shows the stretched static state.

## 4.7 Signature hero art
1. **The flyer-as-UI:** black field; a huge stretched client word in the primary acid colour with a clash-colour outline offset; an op-art tunnel behind it (repeating-radial-gradient); a 12-column hidden grid with info blocks (DATE/PLACE/LINEUP) in mono; an event strip across the bottom; 2 invented sponsor marks.
2. **Warp field:** a wave-distorted checker plane in the vibrating pair (SVG pattern with displacement map), the word above it.
3. **Ticket stack:** overlapping ticket stubs in the pair; one stub bears the brand initial stretched.

## 4.8 Pitfalls
- Neon gradients on dark with chrome 3D text: that is Y2K Chrome or Holo Iridescent.
- Everything warped equally: no resting area, no hierarchy.
- No underlying grid: Eye on Design's own historical sources show the best club work is gridded and sometimes "no DayGlo" restrained (Fabric [V]).
- Thin type on vibrating edges; neon text on neon.
- Stock "acid" fonts and generated blobs with no content; the style faded by 2020 partly because it was overcopied (David Rudnick is called the "most copied touchstone" [V]).
- Drug iconography (acid tabs, pills) and smiley-as-cliché; avoid, especially in client work.
- Copying real flyers or Wipeout logos or team marks: re-make the mechanism with invented marks.

## 4.9 Distinctness
- **Y2K Chrome:** glossy opaque silver, blobby pills, near-black field, chrome as the system. Acid Graphics is flat/saturated, warped and stretched, op-art patterned, dense, information-forward, light or dark; chrome is at most one accent word.
- **Holo Iridescent:** refined, hairline, spectral thin-film on near-black. Acid Graphics is loud, coarse, vibrating pairs, dense.
- **Neo-Brutal Pop / Raw Brutalist / Retro-Futurist:** Neo-Brutal Pop uses chunky borders and pop colours on calm layouts; Acid Graphics breaks letterforms and layers overlays. Retro-Futurist is optimistic, smooth; acid is cynical, distorted.
- **Cyber HUD:** chamfered neon panels and scanlines vs. warp, op-art and ticket strips.

# 5. CYBERSIGILISM (chrome thorns, digital mysticism)

## 5.1 The one idea
Hairline black lines that branch like thorns or circuitry, mirrored and architectural, with silver and cold light. Ornament is the content; legibility is protected by a clear zone around text. Aingelblood's frame: bold, sharp, architectural, mirrored; "Eurotrash" is the fine, wispy, asymmetric cousin [S].

## 5.2 References
1. **CO:CREATE, "The Roots and Aesthetics of Cybersigilism Tattoos"** [V] https://cocreate.ink/post/cybersigilism-tattoo-style. Fine linework with sharp s-curves; mostly black ink; grey wash for shadow; stippling; metallic highlights and white ink on blacked-out areas; occasional highly diluted "barely there" black; motifs (butterflies, daggers, amulets, finials, lightning bolts); lineage from tribal/neo-tribal (Leo Zulueta, Xed Lehead), blackwork, fine line, Y2K; influences black metal, low-fi horror, cyber goth. Term attributed to Aingel Blood.
2. **CO:CREATE, "The Rise of Cybersigilism"** [V] https://www.cocreate.ink/articles/rise-of-cybersigilism-tattoo-trend. Sharp symmetrical linework with dark fills; architectural blueprints between sacred geometry and sci-fi; depends on line consistency, spacing and symmetry; placements follow the body. Artists: Aingelblood, Oz Bardos, Azize Ngo, Musou, Blair Schwartz.
3. **032c, "Cybersigilism: the Forever Trend"** [V] https://magazine.032c.com/magazine/cybersigilism-the-forever-trend. Spiderweb networks, alien vascular systems, Art Nouveau electrical currents; roots in H.R. Giger and metal logos (Christophe Szpajdel); Berlin club scene; Vetements and Balenciaga runways; merch from Drain Gang and others; techniques: symmetry-assist tablets, print-scan-retrace loops, layered collage; critiques of appropriation, copycats, and predicted bad ageing.

## 5.3 Signature mechanisms
- **Needle-thin line.** [V] Strokes 0.75-1.25px at 1x for decorative linework (2px max for primary frames), always tapered where a branch ends. In SVG, tapering needs filled shapes: build each branch as a thin polygon (width 0 at tip, 1.5px at root) or two mirrored curves; stroke-only paths look like wire, not thorn.
- **Sharp s-curves, nodes and spikes.** [V] Branches leave the trunk at 20-40 degrees, curve once with a gentle s, end in a point; small circular nodes (r = 1.5-2px) at junctions; razor "finials" (diamond/lance) at ends.
- **Strict mirror symmetry.** [V] Build half, mirror with `<use transform="scale(-1,1)">`. Add vertical-axis symmetry for frames, radial symmetry (4 or 6-fold) for sigil emblems.
- **Chrome as a thin gradient, not a fill.** [K] Stroke gradient: `#F4F7FA` -> `#9BA4B0` -> `#EAF2FA` -> `#6C7684` with a bright specular spot at 20% of the length; a 4-6px icy blue drop-shadow glow at 25-35% opacity (`#9FD8FF`). Ink blacks stay `#050507`.
- **Grey wash and stippling.** [V] Shading as a 20-30% grey wash under linework and dot stipple at 1px in clusters for depth (CSS radial-gradient dots masked to the shape).
- **White-on-black reversal.** [V] White ink on blacked-out blocks: filled black thorn bands with a white hairline inside.
- **"Barely there" variation.** [V] One ornament at 15-20% opacity as a watermark layer behind content.
- **Diagram annotations.** [K] Fine-line callouts: 0.5px leader lines ending in a node, coordinate pairs in 9px mono, a thin crosshair. The 'blueprint between sacred geometry and sci-fi' reading [V].
- **Blackletter fragments.** [S] Chrome Hearts-like blackletter for one-word marks only.
- **Print-scan-retrace.** [V] Optional tiny wobble in one ornament to avoid sterile vector perfection (a displacement scale of 0.6-1px).

## 5.4 Typography
- Real-world: custom sharp lettering with spiky terminals, blackletter (Chrome Hearts), heavy metal logos (Szpajdel), clean mono/sans for information.
- Google Fonts: blackletter: **Pirata One**, **UnifrakturCook**, **UnifrakturMaguntia**, **Grenze Gotisch**, **New Rocker**; spiky/techno display: **Jacquard 24** (pixel blackletter), **Metal Mania**, **Oxanium**, **Chakra Petch**, **Syncopate**; labels/body: **Space Grotesk**, **JetBrains Mono**, **IBM Plex Mono**, **Jost**, **Tomorrow**, **Rajdhani**; refined contrast: **Italiana**, **Cormorant Unicase**.
- Rules: wordmark/headline in blackletter or a spiky display at 64-120px, tracking -0.01em, set once; all running text in a clean grotesk/mono at 13-15px; labels uppercase mono 9-10px at +0.16em tracking in silver. Headline must be tested at 24px and still read; if it does not, swap the face.
- Size ratios: headline : body about 7-8 : 1, labels 0.65x body.

## 5.5 Colour and brand mapping
- Fixed: obsidian `#050507` (dark) or cold void `#E7ECF2` (light variant), silver ramp `#F4F7FA -> #9BA4B0 -> #5B6572`, and white for specular points.
- **Client primary = the icy glow / light tint** (take its hue, reduce chroma to about 30-45%, raise lightness to about 75-85%, so it reads as cold light: if the client is warm, the glow becomes a pale warm light and the metal stays silver). **Client secondary = grey-wash and shading tint (very low chroma).** **Client accent = one sharp highlight: a node, selected state, progress head, a single crimson/acid spark.** **Dark = obsidian. Light = cold void with graphite lines (invert metal to dark steel).**
- 90% of pixels are black or silver; colour is a light, not a fill.

## 5.6 UI translation (ornament into UI without illegibility)
Rule set: ornament never sits inside a text box; keep a 16-24px clear zone around any text; every ornament has an `aria-hidden` and `pointer-events:none`; text contrast is measured on the plain background without ornament.
- **Header:** a hairline top rule with a mirrored thorn flourish centred above the wordmark (max 24px tall); nav in 10px mono caps; the active item has a small node and an underline that tapers to a point.
- **Hero:** see 5.7.
- **Buttons:** a hairline 1px silver outline rectangle with 4-sided clipped corners (chamfer 6px) plus tiny spike finials at two opposite corners; label mono caps. Hover: outline brightens and a short thorn branch grows from one corner (stroke-dashoffset, 400ms); pressed: fill silver 12%. Primary = filled silver gradient with obsidian text.
- **Tabs / segmented control:** a baseline hairline with tabs as gaps; the active tab is marked by a lance finial above and a node on the baseline; segmented control = three chamfered cells, selected one has a silver fill and a spike at the apex.
- **Toggles:** a hairline lozenge/diamond thumb on a thin track; on = the thumb fills with silver and a short thorn extends from the track end; off = hollow.
- **Inputs:** underline-only field with a tiny node at the left, a thorn "caret" at the right end; focus: the underline extends with a taper and a glow; label in mono caps.
- **Cards/panels:** panels with thorned corner frames (four corner brackets built from branching hairlines, 20-28px) rather than full outlines; inner fill `#0A0B0F` at 70% with a 20% grey-wash gradient at the base; selected = brighter corners.
- **Dense list:** index in mono, item in grotesk 13px, a hairline divider with a node every 5th row, right-aligned status in silver; hover: left spike mark and 4% silver wash. 12-18 rows is fine since type is small and quiet.
- **Progress:** a hairline with a thorn tip that extends as the value increases; ticks every 10%; a node at each milestone; or a radial progress where the ring is a thorn-wreath (a circle of 12 mirrored spikes that light up).
- **Badges:** chamfered mono chips with a spike on one end.
- **Dividers:** a centre-out line that tapers to points with a small branching in the middle (a 5-segment symmetrical flourish).
- **Icons:** 24px grid, 1px stroke, mitred (sharp) joins, spiked terminals; a consistent set of 8-12 icons (home, search, profile, settings) with one thorn notch each.
- **Footer:** a mirrored thorn band across the width at 15% opacity; small mono credits; a tiny sigil emblem centred.
- **Motion (static-safe):** growth by dashoffset (draw-on), shimmer specular highlight crossing once, hover thorn growth; keep durations 300-600ms; reduced motion = all drawn, no shimmer.

## 5.7 Signature hero art
1. **Thorn crest:** a vertically symmetric branching sigil (the brand initial as a trunk; 3 levels of mirrored branches; nodes; a central diamond) drawn in a silver gradient with an icy blue glow, on obsidian, with a faint grey-wash stipple and fine coordinate annotations.
2. **Thorned frame:** a full-width frame made from two mirrored branch systems growing from the corners, centre left empty for the headline; a tiny crosshair and mono labels at the frame midpoints.
3. **Wreath progress:** a radial ring of 24 spikes in two sizes; filled spikes carry the client light colour.
Technique to generate: an L-system or recursive function in JS building tapered polygons (angle 25-35 degrees, length ratio 0.68, depth 4), mirrored once; a deterministic seed per client so it is reproducible.

## 5.8 Pitfalls
- Auto-mirrored generic "tribal" blobs with chunky strokes: it becomes tattoo flash. Lines too thick, too uniform, too symmetric by accident; no visual hierarchy.
- Ornament over text, or text set in a faux-blackletter at body sizes.
- Neon purple and cyan glow overload, which reads as generic cyberpunk (Cyber HUD, Holo).
- AI-generator look: perfectly blended chrome with no node/junction logic and branches that do not connect.
- Cultural issue: neo-tribal borrows from Indigenous patterns; Aingelblood rejects "neo-tribal" and the 032c piece flags appropriation [V]. Do not use named cultural patterns, keep original geometry, and avoid "tribal" in naming/copy.
- The look is already tired: 032c's artists predict it will age badly and is "a style artists are tired of" [V]. Mitigate by using restraint (one crest, one frame) and anchoring in clear UI.
- Using logos or specific artwork of Chrome Hearts or artists: no.

## 5.9 Distinctness
- **Holo Iridescent:** both near-black with thin lines. Holo uses a spectral rim as light. Cybersigilism is monochrome silver with icy blue, and the line is ornament (branching, mirrored) not a halo edge.
- **Cyber HUD:** neon, chamfered, scanlines, readouts. Here: silver, hairline thorns, no scanlines or reticles; chamfer appears only at 6px on buttons.
- **Blueprint:** blue ground and dimension lines; here dark ground and branching ornament.
- **Aurora Noir:** hairline cards on a faded grid with gradients; here no grid, thorn frames.
- **Y2K Chrome:** thick chrome; here hairline chrome.
- **Micrographics:** small mono labels are shared, but the ornament and symmetry differ.
