# 6. INDUSTRIAL HAZARD (the forbidden control room)

## The one insight
In real control rooms, colour is rationed. The high-performance HMI school (ISA-101) paints the healthy process in grey and saves colour, motion and blinking for abnormal states ([ISA blog](https://blog.isa.org/the-high-performance-hmi); [GoJS HMI demo summary via search](https://gojs.net/latest/samples/hmiOperatorScreen)) [S]. Safety signage then ranks danger with a fixed signal-word ladder: DANGER white-on-red, WARNING black-on-orange, CAUTION black-on-yellow, NOTICE white-on-blue ([ANSI Z535](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANSI_Z535)) [S]. A convincing board is therefore a grey, heavy, quiet machine in which every hazard colour carries a rank and a meaning. Hazard colour used as decoration is the failure mode. That rationing is what separates this from Cyber HUD (neon everywhere) and Industrial Device (calm, one-colour, friendly).

## References (premium, real)
1. ANSI Z535 signal-word system. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANSI_Z535. Defines a colour-and-word ladder (DANGER/WARNING/CAUTION/NOTICE) and sub-standards for tags and barricade tape (Z535.5). A ready-made badge hierarchy.
2. OSHA/ANSI colour summaries for signs and tags. https://www.sjsu.edu/up/docs/risk/info/what_colors_mark_hazards.pdf and https://www.valmet.com/insights/articles/up-and-running/reliability/FRSafetyColors/. Red = stop/danger, orange = warning, yellow = caution/physical hazard. Safety alert triangle sits left of the signal word at least as tall as the word [S, per search summary].
3. Lockout/tagout tags and "do not operate" labels (McMaster-Carr catalogue). https://www.mcmaster.com/do-not-operate-labels. Lockout identifies equipment removed from service that may not be operated; tagout is used when equipment cannot be shut down [S, per search summary of OSHA 1910.147 usage]. Gives you a permission-state vocabulary for toggles.
4. ISA-101 "high performance HMI". https://blog.isa.org/the-high-performance-hmi and https://emersonexchange365.com/iiot-digital-transformation/b/weblog/posts/up-your-productivity-and-safety-with-high-performance-hmi-design-white-paper. Grey baseline, restricted line styles, 2D equipment, no shading or gradients, four-level display hierarchy from overview to diagnostics.
5. ISA-18.1 annunciator sequences. https://predig.com/indicatorpage/isa-alarm-sequencing-nutshell and https://webstore.ansi.org/standards/isa/isa181979r2004. Five channel states: normal, alarm, acknowledged alarm, return-to-normal, acknowledged return-to-normal; unacknowledged alarm flashes, acknowledging turns it steady [S]. A true state machine for status lights and list rows.
6. Chernobyl RBMK unit-4 mnemonic panel (lamp legend). https://orau.org/health-physics-museum/collection/accidents/light-cover-from-chernobyl.html. White lamps = fuel channels, yellow = channels with power-density probes, green = control-rod channels; red later added for fast scram rods [S]. Shows a real panel using three or four lamp colours as a legend, with a grid of identical lamp cells. Note: this source describes the lamp legend only, not overall layout.
7. McMaster-Carr site. https://www.mcmaster.com/ with analysis at https://www.designnews.com/motion-control/the-perfect-site. Mostly grey, accents of green and yellow, no carousels, popups or banners, even images in greyscale; catalogue-page density; every part within two clicks [S]. The best living example of dense industrial information UI.
8. Off-White (Virgil Abloh) industrial graphics. https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/virgil-abloh-off-white-quotation-marks/ and https://lrb.co.uk/blog/2021/december/figures-of-speech. Diagonal hazard-style stripes, quotation-marked labels on ordinary things ("quote-marked" captions), Helvetica as a neutral voice, emergency-style instruction text, oversized words as the main graphic [S]. Also an arrow derived from airport wayfinding signage (Glasgow, per Diet Prada via the search summary).
9. Heron Preston x NYC Sanitation. https://www.wmagazine.com/story/fashion-weeks-most-unlikely-pairing-heron-preston-and-the-new-york-department-of-sanitation and https://hypebeast.com/2016/9/heron-preston-dsny-pricing. Archival department insignia and hi-vis orange reworked as streetwear; shows official-institution graphics (patches, department logos) as a luxury signal [S].
10. Game UI. Control's Federal Bureau of Control: brutalist architecture plus bold sans title cards in a highway-sign register that feels "deeply sixties", with a round C echoing the O above it (https://kotaku.com/the-fonts-in-control-make-its-world-feel-way-creepier-1837714580; https://gameinformer.com/2019/03/27/the-mesmerizing-art-behind-control). Alien: Isolation: clunky, "broken-feeling" motion tracker as a physical tool, health bar styled like a camera battery indicator, references limited to pre-1979 material (https://www.dualshockers.com/latest-developer-diary-for-alien-isolation-details-the-games-lo-fi-sci-fi-design/). Death Stranding: logistics-heavy UI with a VCR-like mono for numbers (https://fontsinuse.com/uses/67648/death-stranding-video-game; font attribution there is unconfirmed).

## Signature mechanisms (concrete)
- Ground is mid-to-dark concrete grey with slight tonal steps between panel levels (overview / control / detail), the ISA-101 idea of different greys per display level ([Tatsoft example, vendor convention](https://docs.tatsoft.com/display/FX/ISA-101+HMI+Compliance+How-to)) [S]. [R] Add a faint cast-concrete noise (SVG feTurbulence at 3-5% opacity) and shuttering seams (1px dark line every 240px).
- Hazard stripes: alternating yellow and black at about 45 degrees with roughly equal band widths ([Norwegian workplace regulation, a national rule, not OSHA](https://arbeidstilsynet.no/en/laws-and-regulations/regulations/the-workplace-regulations/6/section5-16)) [S]. OSHA 1910.144 gives colour meaning (yellow for physical hazards) but no stripe geometry in what I could find ([Brady floor-marking guide](https://punchout.acklandsgrainger.com/ec/pdf/Brady-Floor-Marking-Reference-Guide.pdf)) [S]. [R] CSS: `repeating-linear-gradient(-45deg, #111 0 10px, #F2C500 10px 20px)`; pitch 20-24px at large, 12px at small. Use stripes only on edges, thresholds and guards of dangerous controls, never as page background. A 6-8px stripe rail above one panel is enough; two stripe areas on a board is the maximum.
- Signal-word panel: a solid colour band holding the word in caps, a triangle glyph left at word height, then a white or grey message body. This is the card/alert anatomy [S, ANSI Z535].
- Serial and stencil identification: every panel carries a plate with model, serial, voltage/rating, date code. [R] Format `MODEL CR-7 / S/N 004-1187-B / 24 V DC / MFD 03-26`, set in mono 10-11px, tracked +8%.
- Steel panel borders: [R] 2px dark outer line, 1px light inner bevel line, four corner screws as 6px circles with a cross slot. Rivet rows on the header plate.
- Oversized status lights: [R] 28-40px lamp "cabochons" with a darker ring, a specular highlight (white radial at 30%/25%), and a glow only when lit. The unlit state is a dim, desaturated lens, which keeps the board grey until something is wrong (ISA-101).
- Mimic diagram: [R] a simple 2D process schematic (tank, pipe, valve, pump) in 2-3px strokes with no shading, per ISA-101's 2D, no-gradient rule.
- Wayfinding: Off-White-style quoted labels and arrow, and Control-style bold sans title cards used as zone names [S].
- Density: McMaster-like dense lists, hard row dividers, right-aligned numerics in mono. Scale contrast: giant condensed zone numbers (e.g. "04") against 10px labels.

## Typography
- Class: heavy condensed grotesque for zone names and signal words; DIN-like or highway-sign sans for labels; mono for readouts and serials; optional stencil for equipment IDs.
- Google Fonts approximations [R]: display `Big Shoulders Display` (700-900), `Barlow Condensed` (600-800), `Oswald`, `Anton` for 120px+ numerals, `Saira Extra Condensed`; stencil `Big Shoulders Stencil Display` or `Saira Stencil One` or `Allerta Stencil`; labels `Barlow` / `Barlow Semi Condensed` / `Archivo Narrow`; readouts `IBM Plex Mono`, `B612 Mono` (made for aircraft cockpit displays), `Share Tech Mono`.
- Case and size [R]: ALL CAPS for anything with a rank. Tracking +6 to +10% on 10-12px labels, -1 to 0% on giant. Ratio about 8:1 between zone numeral (96-160px) and label (11-12px). Headlines are built as: ZONE NUMBER (giant) + zone name (condensed 28-40px) + one-line mono spec under it.

## Colour: roles and brand mapping
- Fixed [R]: concrete greys (about #8E9092 mid, #5F6264 dark, #2A2C2E deep, #C9CACB light), black #111, and the signal ladder. Proposed starting hazard values: yellow #F2C500, orange #FF6A13, red #D3202A, notice blue #1E5FA8. The ladder must keep its word-to-colour pairs even when re-hued.
- Brand mapping [R]: the client primary becomes machine enamel, the colour a manufacturer paints the housing (the way a yellow excavator reads as the maker's colour). Use it on the nameplate, header plate, main button face and one big zone numeral. The client secondary becomes the "RUN / ARMED" lamp and active-state fill. Accent is allowed on one callout only. Dark/light brand colours become the black and the panel paper.
- Collision rule [R]: if the brand primary falls in the yellow-orange-red band, keep the hazard ladder at reduced chroma and let the brand be enamel on large areas; hazard stripes then go black on a desaturated stripe tint, so the board never shows two competing "danger" oranges.

## UI translation
- Buttons [R]: slab keys with a visible plinth. Primary = enamel face, 2px dark border, 3px bottom edge, depress 2px on press. Dangerous action = guarded: a hazard-stripe rail on the button's lower edge plus a "hold 1.0 s" fill. Emergency = mushroom button (circle, red, black collar), one per board.
- Tabs / segmented control [R]: a bank of rectangular push-lamps (annunciator tiles) where selected is lit steady; each tile has an engraved legend, 2px gaps like tile seams.
- Toggles [R]: toggle-switch with metal lever (a rectangle slot with a dark rounded lever), plus a guard cover for the critical one. States read as `OFF / ON` text on the plate, not colour alone.
- Inputs [R]: recessed field with 1px inner shadow, mono value, unit suffix on a separate plate (`kPa`, `rpm`). Invalid value = amber CAUTION band under the field with a triangle glyph.
- Cards / panels [R]: steel-framed modules with a nameplate strip; signal-word headers for alert cards.
- Lists [R]: row = serial (mono) / name / state lamp / value; ack'd states steady, unack'd flashing (static-safe: show a lamp ring instead of flashing).
- Progress [R]: segmented 24-cell bar (lamp cells) or analog gauge arc; completed cells white, not brand colour, until threshold.
- Badges [R]: four signal-word chips plus LOCKED OUT and TAGGED badges (a tag shape with punched hole and string line).
- Dividers [R]: stripe rail (rare), double hairline (common), screw-row (header).
- Header / footer [R]: header is a nameplate with plant ID, shift code and a clock; footer is a legal-style label block (standard references, revision letter, "authorised personnel only").
- Motion (static-safe) [R]: annunciator flash = 1 Hz opacity step, no easing, replaced by a steady ring under `prefers-reduced-motion`; switch throw 80ms linear; no springs.

## Hero art ideas (SVG/CSS only)
1. "Interlock": a huge condensed zone numeral (e.g. 07) cut by a diagonal stripe band that only crosses the lower third, with a guarded lamp row (five lamps, one lit in the brand secondary) and a nameplate with serial.
2. "Mimic board": 2D plant schematic of tank, pipes, valve and gauge in 2px strokes on concrete grey; one pipe segment filled with the brand primary as the "live" line; callout tags quoted in Off-White style.
3. "Lockout hasp": a steel hasp and padlock drawn flat, with a danger tag hanging on string reading `DO NOT OPERATE / TAG 0412 / J. KOWAL`. Tag uses signal-word anatomy.

## Pitfalls
- Hazard stripes used as wallpaper or on every card: reads as a Halloween costume. Max two stripe zones per screen.
- Everything neon or glowing: that becomes Cyber HUD. Lamps glow only when lit.
- Soft rounded glass cards and pastel greys: that becomes Industrial Device or Soft Form.
- Illegible orange-on-yellow: always black text on yellow or orange, white on red or blue (ANSI) [S].
- Fake "Chernobyl" Cyrillic or Soviet iconography: avoid; it is a real disaster and a clichéd shortcut.
- Rust and grunge filters: dirt textures make a board look like a game mod; keep surfaces clean and officially maintained, with the menace coming from the hierarchy.
- Alert fatigue: if everything is warning, nothing is. The board should have exactly one DANGER element.

## Distinctness vs existing aesthetics
- Industrial Device (Teenage Engineering/Braun): calm, light aluminium, soft radius, friendly single accent. Hazard is dark concrete, heavy steel, ranked warnings, lockout states.
- Cyber HUD: neon on black, thin lines, glow. Hazard: matte, thick strokes, colour only when abnormal.
- Raw Brutalist: unstyled web-native brutalism. Hazard is a designed institutional signage system with a rule-based colour ladder.
- Retro-Futurist: 70s console with warmth and LCD. Hazard is plant-room severity with serials and tags.
- Blueprint / Micrographics: drawing-sheet logic. Hazard is operating-panel logic (state, rank, permission).

# 7. RADICAL MEMPHIS (the experimental Italian furniture showroom as an app)

## The one insight
Memphis is a laminate-surface logic, not a random-shapes style. The group used cheap plastic laminate (kitchen-counter material) as the primary surface, mixed it with hardwood, lacquer and brass, and let pattern be the object's skin ([Wallpaper* guide](https://www.wallpaper.com/design-interiors/memphis-design-group-definitive-guide); [Design Museum](https://designmuseum.org/discover-design/all-stories/memphis-group-awful-or-awesome); [Design Museum on Sottsass](https://designmuseum.org/designers/ettore-sottsass)) [S]. Objects are assembled from stacked primitives with diagonals that give them an almost anthropomorphic stance. Black-and-white squiggle patterns (Sottsass's Bacterio) act as the neutral that holds the saturated colours together ([Bonhams](https://bonhams.shorthandstories.com/collecting-memphis/)) [S]. So a good board is a catalogue of furniture-like objects wearing patterned skins, not a sticker sheet.

## References (premium, real)
1. Design Museum, "Memphis Group: awful or awesome?" https://designmuseum.org/discover-design/all-stories/memphis-group-awful-or-awesome and https://designmuseum.org/memphis. Bright clashing palettes, geometric forms, bold patterns, laminate; influences of Art Deco and Pop Art; ridiculed then revived; group formed 1980, debuted Milan 18 Sept 1981 [S].
2. Memphis Group overview. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis_Group. Names, Carlton room divider (Sottsass, 1981, wood veneer and plastic laminate), "shotgun wedding between Bauhaus and Fisher-Price" reception line [S].
3. Wallpaper* definitive guide. https://www.wallpaper.com/design-interiors/memphis-design-group-definitive-guide. Colours (Carlton combines green, yellow, blue, red, pink), materials, named pieces: Carlton, Tahiti lamp, Casablanca, Big Sur sofa (Shire, 1986), Arizona rug (Du Pasquier), Super lamp (Bedin), Plaza (Graves). Place-name titles. After 1985 the palette darkened [S].
4. Bonhams "Collecting Memphis". https://bonhams.shorthandstories.com/collecting-memphis/. Bacterio, Abet Laminati as funder and surface supplier, Casablanca sideboard as a laminate showpiece [S; the Bacterio-origin story is single-blog, treat as unverified].
5. Nathalie Du Pasquier. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathalie_Du_Pasquier; https://patternobserver.com/?p=24882; https://frieze.com/article/nathalie-du-pasquier-my-influences. Youngest founding member, responsible for much of the group's surface pattern; collage of childlike motifs, geometric forms, squiggles and blots; patterns spread to textiles, carpets, laminates and small goods [S]. Later HAY bags (2013) and an American Apparel line (2014) per the Design Museum page.
6. Peter Shire (Big Sur sofa, Bel Air armchair, Brazil desk) via the Wallpaper* guide. Sculptural colour-block geometry rather than surface pattern; Sottsass praised his teapots [S, per search summary; direct Shire sources not found].
7. We Are The Mutants, "No Powerful Idea Lasts Long". https://wearethemutants.com/2018/09/20/no-powerful-idea-lasts-long-the-memphis-group-and-the-1980s-look/. Context on how the look went from showroom to 1980s mass culture [S, not fully read].
8. Memphis revival in packaging and brands. https://www.packworld.com/home/article/21784121/brands-reinvent-the-memphis-retro-design-trend and https://mymodernmet.com/what-is-memphis-design/. Contemporary revivals mix primaries with mellow teals, soft pinks and greys; Temescal Brewing and Who Gives a Crap cited [S, trade-press level].
9. CAUTIONARY: Buck's Facebook Alegria system. https://buck.co/work/facebook-alegria and https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/dont-worry-these-gangley-armed-cartoons-are-here-to-protect-you-from-big-tech/. Flat geometric illustration system (oversized limbs, non-natural skin colours); widely nicknamed "Corporate Memphis" and criticised as generic [S]. Use it as the model of what to avoid.
10. Memphis Milano objects at auction and dealer sites (e.g. https://www.1stdibs.com/furniture/lighting/table-lamps/memphis-milano-tahiti-lamp-ettore-sottsass/id-f_46044222/). Good for object proportions: Tahiti lamp has a Bacterio base, yellow stem, black foot, pink rotating head and red beak [S].

## Signature mechanisms (concrete)
- Pattern is a material [S]. Build a library of 5-6 laminates and apply them as fills to panels, buttons and list rows, the way a showroom applies them to cabinets. [R] Suggested set: (a) Bacterio-type black squiggles on white; (b) terrazzo speckle (random small polygons in 4 colours on off-white); (c) tight black/white stripe or checker (8px); (d) dot grid; (e) zig-zag stripe; (f) large-scale solid with one inset pattern strip.
- Objects are stacked primitives [S]. [R] Controls and cards as assembled solids: a circle on a rectangle on a trapezoid, off-axis by 3-8 degrees. Cards should look like cabinets or room dividers (a tall panel with a stepped top), not rounded rectangles.
- Palette clash with a rule [S, Wallpaper*]: saturated primaries sit next to pink and green; black-and-white pattern calms them. [R] Rule: at most 4 hues + black/white per screen; 60% of area in 1-2 flats, 30% in pattern, 10% in a screamer.
- Diagonals and asymmetry [S]: nothing sits on a 90 degree stance for long. [R] Slant one edge of a panel 6-12 degrees; hero composition is asymmetric with one big circle off the canvas edge.
- Place-name titles [S]: Arizona, Tahiti, Brazil, Casablanca. [R] Name sections and states like products: "Casablanca / Settings", "Tahiti / Play".
- Hard shadows: acceptable but differentiated. [R] Use a second-hue offset sheet (e.g. a pink duplicate shape 8px down-right) instead of black offset shadows, and no black outlines. Memphis objects carry flat colour planes, not sticker strokes.
- Springy motion [R]: 180-260ms overshoot (cubic-bezier(.34,1.56,.64,1)) on press; shapes wobble 2 degrees on hover; reduced-motion = instant state change.

## Typography
- Period typography was geometric and playful: Futura-style and Avant Garde-style sans, often mixed in size, stacked and set big (Memphis graphics circa 1981-86) [R, from general knowledge; not sourced here].
- Google Fonts approximations [R]: display `Unbounded` or `Syne` (800) for wide geometric; `Bagel Fat One`, `Lilita One`, `Climate Crisis`, `Bowlby One`, `Shrikhand` for fat playful; geometric body `Jost` (Futura-like), `League Spartan`, `Outfit`; small labels `DM Mono` or `Space Grotesk`.
- Case [R]: mixed. Display in lowercase or title case; labels uppercase at 11px with +6% tracking. Headlines: one fat word at 96-160px set tight (-3%), a second line rotated 90 degrees or set on a curved or slanted baseline; never centre-aligned paragraphs.

## Colour: roles and brand mapping
- Reference palette in good examples [S]: cobalt, tomato red, lemon yellow, pink, green, black, white; later Memphis darkened toward smoky tones after 1985 [S]. Modern revival adds teal and soft pink [S].
- Fixed [R]: black and white used for pattern and linework; an off-white ground (#F6F1E7); at least one pastel (pink or mint) and one primary beside any brand colour.
- Brand mapping [R]: client primary = the dominant flat (the cabinet colour); secondary = the second laminate (pattern accent); accent = the screamer circle or dot; client dark = the black in squiggles; client light = ground. If the brand palette is muted, add two "borrowed" saturated colours for the pattern library and say so on the board; pure brand-only Memphis tends to look washed. Terrazzo speckles can use all brand colours at small area, which is the safest way to inject brand variety.

## UI translation
- Buttons [R]: circles and pills with a pattern or flat fill; primary = large circle (56-72px) on a square plinth in another hue; secondary = pill with squiggle underline. Press = squash 6%.
- Tabs / segmented [R]: a row of geometric tokens (circle / triangle / square) instead of text-only tabs; selected token enlarges 1.15 and takes the screamer colour. Or a checkerboard-edged strip with the active cell filled.
- Toggles [R]: a pill track in laminate pattern with a solid circle knob; ON = knob jumps to the end and the track pattern flips to a second laminate.
- Inputs [R]: fields as thick-edged slabs (6px bottom border in accent) with a triangle or circle label tab; focus = a squiggle underline animates in.
- Cards [R]: cabinet silhouettes: stepped tops, one corner cut at 45 degrees, pattern strip on the side, second-hue offset sheet behind.
- Lists [R]: each row has a tiny primitive icon (circle/triangle/square/zigzag) as a "product swatch", a place-name title and a mono value; alternate rows use two pastel flats.
- Progress [R]: row of beads (circles) filling, or a squiggle line that draws itself; a zig-zag fill bar.
- Badges [R]: small solid shapes with text, tilted -4 degrees.
- Dividers [R]: squiggle line, row of dots, black-and-white checker strip (12px), zig-zag.
- Header / footer [R]: asymmetric nav with a big wordmark on a colour block and nav links scattered at different heights; footer as a patterned base plinth with a squiggle edge.
- Motion (static-safe) [R]: shapes bob 4px, wobble on hover; under reduced-motion nothing moves and the same composition remains.

## Hero art ideas (SVG/CSS only)
1. "Showroom": an isometric-ish assembled object (a Carlton-like totem) built from 6-8 coloured primitives with three different laminates, standing on a terrazzo floor tile, with a second-hue offset sheet behind.
2. "Laminate swatch wall": a 4 x 3 grid of laminate sample chips at different tilts, each named like a product ("Bacterio-type", "Terrazzo", "Dots"), with one chip pulled out and enlarged.
3. "Lamp": a Tahiti-like lamp rendered from circle, stem, base and beak primitives, with the pattern base in black/white and the head in the brand accent.

## Pitfalls
- Corporate Memphis: lanky flat people, gradient-free blobs and squiggle confetti sprinkled on a white SaaS page [S, Alegria critique]. Memphis lives in objects and materials, not in confetti.
- Random scatter: triangles, plus signs and squiggles dropped without a pattern system look like a stock pack. Put patterns on surfaces and structure the composition.
- Too many hues: more than four plus black/white becomes noise; unify with pattern.
- Neo-Brutal Pop clone: thick black outlines, sticker stacks and black offset shadows on a grid canvas. Remove outlines; use pattern fills and coloured offset sheets.
- Groovy 70s clone: warm earthy rounded type, rainbows, flower shapes. Memphis is cooler, angular and plastic.
- Illegible pattern under text: put text only on flat fields or white labels.

## Distinctness vs existing aesthetics
- Neo-Brutal Pop: sticker plus outline plus offset-shadow grid. Memphis: laminate patterns, furniture-like objects, no outlines, coloured sheets.
- Groovy 70s: warm, round, hippy type. Memphis: cool plastic, diagonals, black/white patterns.
- Bauhaus Poster: strict primary geometry and a grid. Memphis: asymmetry and pattern, deliberately "bad taste".
- Y2K Chrome / Holo: glossy gradients. Memphis: flat matte planes.
- Riso Print: overprint textures. Memphis: clean flats and printed laminate.

# 8. BIOMECHANICAL GOTHIC (the living machine)

## The one insight
The strongest biomechanical work uses one material and a rhythm, not gore. Giger's bar in Gruyères has double vertebral arches crossing a vaulted ceiling, bone-coloured furniture, chairs whose backrests end in pelvis shapes, stone slabs with hieroglyphs, and everything cast from a stone-like synthetic so the listed castle's atmosphere is preserved ([Giger Museum bar](https://www.hrgigermuseum.com/gigerbar)) [S]. Scorn's team starts from many parts of human anatomy and then morphs, combines and exaggerates them, "not always about functionality" but forms that suit what they want to express ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/au/behind-the-beautifully-grotesque-art-of-scorn/); [Unreal interview](https://www.unrealengine.com/developer-interviews/creating-the-unreal-dystopian-surrealism-and-biomechanical-horrors-of-scorn)) [S]. In interface terms: build everything from a few repeated structural modules (rib, vertebra, tendon, valve) in one tarnished material, and make the technology and the biology read as the same object.

## References (premium, real)
1. H. R. Giger official biography. https://www.hrgiger.com/bio.htm. Studied architecture and industrial design in Zurich; airbrush discovery led to the biomechanical dreamscapes; Necronomicon (1977) was the visual inspiration for Ridley Scott's Alien [S].
2. Giger Museum Bar, Gruyères. https://www.hrgigermuseum.com/gigerbar (also https://www.graubuenden.ch/en/gastronomy/giger-bar for the Chur bar, opened 1992). Vertebral double arches, bone-toned furniture, cast synthetic stone, womb-like cavern [S].
3. Alien (1979) production design. https://www.domusweb.it/en/news/2025/09/19/alien-design-nostromo.amp.html and https://comic-watch.com/movies/spfx-part-13-alien. Giger credited on the creature, the derelict and its planet; corridors with scaly projections and pipes like vessels; Roger Christian built sets from scrap parts and used mirrors to lengthen corridors [S, per search summaries]. The Space Jockey wall was airbrushed by hand [S].
4. Scorn (Ebb Software). https://www.unrealengine.com/developer-interviews/creating-the-unreal-dystopian-surrealism-and-biomechanical-horrors-of-scorn and https://www.pcgamer.com/au/behind-the-beautifully-grotesque-art-of-scorn/. Beksiński for decaying structures, Giger for the thematic space; weapons as living organisms with mouths; mechanics built from the same themes as the art [S]. I could not verify claims about its HUD-less design.

## Signature mechanisms (concrete)
- Single-material monochrome [S, Giger Bar]: all surfaces appear cast from one pour. [R] Use a charcoal base (#15130F), bone (#D8D0BC), tarnished metal (#6F6A5C mid, #8E8670 highlight), with texture as the only variation. No pure white.
- Ribbing as rhythm [S, Alien corridors and Giger Bar]: [R] repeating tapering arcs with irregular spacing (e.g. 14px, 16px, 15px, 18px). Implement as SVG `<path>` ribs along a spine, with stroke widths varying 3-9px and rounded caps. Spacing varies slowly so it reads as grown.
- Vertebral column as navigation spine [R]: a vertical column of stacked vertebra shapes (rounded trapezoid with a hole) is the nav; the active vertebra is bone-bright and rotates 3 degrees; sub-items branch as nerve lines.
- Tubing: [R] 8-14px tubes drawn as parallel strokes with a lighter centre highlight and a darker edge, joining panels with swollen "valve" bulges (ellipses with ridges).
- Skeletal arches as frames [R]: card frames are a pointed or elliptical arch (top only) with a double rim; the second rim is offset 6px and ribbed with short transverse lines; bottom corners are squared.
- Grown components [R]: no perfectly circular items; every control has a slightly asymmetric pill shape with a faint seam line and a few pores (small dark ellipses). Use slow blob-morph border-radius on a 6-9s loop for "alive" states.
- Tonal range [R]: very dark ground, controlled highlights on edges only (rim light), as in airbrushed Giger work. Shading is soft gradient on tubes, but flat on text.
- Scale: [S, Nihei] the structure is vast and the user tiny. [R] Use huge cropped spine and rib structures bleeding off the edges; small text blocks sit inside.
- Glyphs: [R] hieroglyph-like marks (Giger Bar floor slabs [S]) as decoration only, invented from line segments, never real scripts.

## Typography
- Class: high-contrast or inscriptional serif at light weight for titles; extended technical sans for small labels (film-era corporate/hardware feel, as in the Microgramma lineage noted at https://severance.wiki/typography); mono for annotations.
- Google Fonts approximations [R]: display `Cormorant` / `Cormorant Garamond` (300-500), `Cinzel` (inscriptional), `Italiana`, `Bodoni Moda`; labels `Michroma` or `Syncopate` (extended), `Tenor Sans`; mono `IBM Plex Mono`, `Xanh Mono`; body `Spectral` or `Newsreader`.
- Case and size [R]: titles in small caps or all caps with +10-14% tracking at 32-56px, weight 300-400; section numbers as roman numerals; labels 10-11px extended caps +16% tracking. Avoid blackletter and fantasy gothic fonts (Pirata One etc.), which push toward costume-store metal.
- Headline construction [R]: one word per line, left-aligned on the rib spine, with the first letter in bone and the rest in tarnished metal.

## Colour: roles and brand mapping
- Fixed [R]: charcoal/near-black ground, bone neutrals, tarnished metal mids. Tone is desaturated and warm-leaning (browns/greens in the grey), never blue-black "tech".
- Brand mapping [R]: client primary becomes a "lit sinew" accent: it appears only as thin glows or fills inside the structure (progress, active vertebra, a single eye-like lamp), always desaturated 15-25% and darkened so it looks bio-luminescent rather than UI-bright. Secondary = tarnish tint (verdigris or brass chosen from the brand's nearest colour). Accent = one wet-looking gloss spot (small, <2% of area). Dark/light brand colours replace charcoal/bone when they are close in hue; if the brand is a bright pastel, drop its lightness and let it appear only in the glow.
- Never use saturated red as the default accent (reads as gore) unless it is the brand.

## UI translation
- Buttons [R]: bone pill with a seam, ribbed grip lines on the left edge; hover = rib lines spread 1px; press = dilation (scale .97) with inner shadow. Primary has a glowing "sinew" line under the label.
- Tabs / segmented [R]: vertebrae row; selected vertebra is raised and lit; segmented control is a short spine with separators as discs.
- Toggles [R]: a sphincter-like iris (circle that contracts) or a bone lever in a ribbed groove; ON = glow fill travels through the groove.
- Inputs [R]: recessed channel (like a groove cast in stone), caret as a thin glowing filament, labels in extended caps above.
- Cards [R]: arch frames with double rim and ribbing at the shoulders; header strip with roman numeral.
- Lists [R]: rows as vertebrae stack: a number disc on the left (roman), name, tendon line running to a right-hand value; rows separated by sinew hairlines of varying opacity.
- Progress and loading [R]: a pulse travelling along a tube (gradient moving along the stroke), or ring that "breathes" (scale 1.00 to 1.03 and opacity .85 to 1 over 4-6s, ease-in-out). Static-safe: show the lit portion as a segment of the tube; `prefers-reduced-motion` freezes it.
- Sliders [R]: a tube with a bone bead as the thumb; track has ribs that tighten near the thumb.
- Badges [R]: pill with pores; status words: DORMANT / AWAKE / FEEDING (neutral lexicon, no gore).
- Dividers [R]: spine line with irregular vertebra marks; a tapered double line.
- Header / footer [R]: header = arched lintel with wordmark in the keystone; footer = base of the columns with a rib band and roman-numeral legal text.
- Motion [R]: slow (3-8s) cycles, easing in-out, never bouncy; hover = slight swelling. Reduced-motion: static glows.

## Hero art ideas (SVG/CSS only)
1. "The Nave": symmetric receding rib arches (7-9 arches shrinking toward a vanishing point), each a double-stroke arc with transverse ribs, in charcoal/bone/tarnished metal; a single brand-colour lamp at the vanishing point.
2. "Spine Index": a giant cropped vertebral column running down the left, with tubing branching to labelled nodes, a thin brand-coloured pulse traveling the spine.
3. "Specimen Plate": a museum-plate style fold-out showing an invented valve-organ made of repeated rib pairs, tube bundles and a ring, with measured callouts in extended caps and roman-numeral figure labels. Keeps it scientific, not gory.

## Pitfalls
- Gore, flesh textures, teeth, blood-red wounds: out of scope and cheap.
- Direct Giger/Alien imagery, xenomorph silhouettes or the Space Jockey: copyright and clichéd. Use rhythm and structure, not iconography.
- Blackletter/fantasy fonts and skulls: reads as a metal band merch page.
- Glow-green or cyan highlights: becomes Cyber HUD or sci-fi lab. Keep highlights bone and dull.
- Everything curved: needs a few hard, straight structural elements for contrast (Nihei's architecture [S]).
- Low contrast: dark-on-dark text. Keep body text at least 4.5:1 on charcoal; the mood lives in decoration, not in text.
- Slow loops that never stop: respect reduced-motion and keep animation limited to one or two elements.

## Distinctness vs existing aesthetics
- Aurora Noir: glowing dark gradients and luxury. Biomech: matte, single-material, structural, no aurora.
- Cyber HUD: neon thin-line tech. Biomech: thick, organic, grown.
- Raw Brutalist: concrete and plain HTML. Biomech: organic repetition and arches.
- Blob Tracking / Clay: soft blobs. Biomech: ribbed, skeletal and tubular with hard bone edges.
- Editorial Serif: a calm light layout. Biomech shares a serif but sits in dark, structural frames.

# 9. PIRATE BROADCAST (the intercepted transmission)

## The one insight
Broadcast graphics were diagnostic and procedural. Test Card F's every element has a job: colour blocks at the sides would tear the picture if sync was off, closely spaced lines check focus from centre to edge, the greyscale checks contrast and brightness, the black bar on white reveals ringing, castellations along the edges reveal setup problems, and the child at the centre was chosen so wrong skin tones would show ([Test Card F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_Card_F)) [S]. Teletext pages were a 24-row by 40-column fixed grid with eight colours and block graphics built from the same cells as text, addressed by three-digit page numbers with four colour-coded Fastext buttons ([Typeroom](https://www.typeroom.eu/article/teletext); [WeTransfer](https://wepresent.wetransfer.com/story/teletext-creative-legacy/); [Teletext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teletext)) [S]. A pirate-broadcast UI therefore gets its authority from a rigid, technical frame (grid, page numbers, bars, timecode), and its menace from intrusion into that frame. The 1987 Max Headroom hijacking interrupted a real newscast for about 17 seconds ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Headroom_signal_hijacking)) [S]. Captain Midnight's 1986 hijack laid a message over SMPTE bars ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Midnight_broadcast_signal_intrusion)) [S].

## Signature mechanisms (concrete)
- The frame is the brand [S]. [R] The whole board sits inside a 4:3 or 16:9 "screen" with rounded CRT corners (radius 3-5%), a faint vignette, and a bezel or safe-area guides (title-safe 90%, action-safe 93%) drawn as thin dashed lines.
- Teletext grid [S]: 40 columns x 24 rows, monospace, each cell the same size. [R] Build the layout in CSS grid with `grid-template-columns: repeat(40, 1ch)`; row 0 is the header strip: `P100  PIRATE TEXT  100  SAT 11 OCT  23:41:07`. Colour-coded headline rows: cyan headings, yellow lead lines, white body, green positive, red alert, magenta special, blue background blocks.
- Control-character gaps [S, Typeroom]: when the text colour changes mid-row, leave a one-cell black gap. [R] This visual quirk marks authenticity; apply it to label rows.
- Double-height headings [R, standard teletext behaviour from general knowledge, not confirmed in the fetched pages]: render headline text at 2x vertical scale, still on the cell grid.
- Block mosaic graphics [S]: 2 x 3 sixel blocks. [R] Draw logos, bars and big numerals as chunky mosaics using a 2x3 sub-cell grid in SVG or CSS grid.
- Page-number navigation [S]: 3-digit page numbers, front page 100, four Fastext colour buttons at the bottom row (red/green/yellow/blue). [R] Make the nav bar the Fastext row: `RED news  GREEN sport  YELLOW weather  BLUE tune`.
- Test pattern as hero [S]: colour bars, castellations, greyscale, gratings. [R] Build an original test card (do not copy Test Card F's girl or clown): bars, a centre circle with crosshair, greyscale ramp, grating lines, corner castellations and a station ident (`ST-9 / 4.2 MHz / CH 43`).
- Timecode and station ident [S]: HH:MM:SS:FF readouts. [R] Persistent top-left `REC ● 23:41:07:12`, top-right channel number, bottom-left lower-third (chyron) with a slanted edge.
- Interruption as state [S, Max Headroom, Captain Midnight, Local 58]: [R] the UI shows an "official" programme view, then cuts to bars or noise with a message in plain mono. The static board version shows both states side by side: normal programme and intrusion frame.
- Analogue artefacts [S, Alien: Isolation]: scanlines, a rolling tracking bar, slight horizontal jitter on one row, chroma bleed (2px offset of red and cyan), vignette, film grain. [R] Keep intensity low: scanline opacity 6-10%, chroma split 1-2px.
- Density: teletext pages are dense and left-aligned; a 40-column text block with 20-24 lines is the norm [S].

## Typography
- Class: blocky bitmap/teletext face for body, condensed heavy sans for chyrons and station names, mono for timecodes.
- Google Fonts approximations [R]: bitmap `DotGothic16`, `Silkscreen`, `Pixelify Sans`, `VT323` (terminal, not teletext), `Doto` (dot matrix) where available; chyron `Oswald`, `Bebas Neue`, `Barlow Condensed`, `Anton`; broadcast numerals `Archivo Black`, `Rubik Mono One`; timecode `Share Tech Mono`, `IBM Plex Mono`.
- Case and size [R]: ALL CAPS throughout; base cell 16-20px; headline double-height at 2x; chyron 28-40px bold condensed with +2% tracking; station ident huge (120-200px) as a block numeral. Authentic teletext glyphs are not in Google Fonts; draw the hero numerals and wordmark as mosaics.

## Colour: roles and brand mapping
- Teletext's palette (eight): black, red, green, yellow, blue, magenta, cyan, white [S for "eight colours"; names from standard Mode 7 usage]. SMPTE bars: white, yellow, cyan, green, magenta, red, blue plus black/PLUGE [S].
- Roles in good examples [R]: black ground; one phosphor hue for text (CRT amber about #FFB000 or washed cyan about #8FD3D0); white for body; red only for REC and alerts; blue for header blocks.
- Fixed [R]: dark ground, limited 6-8 colours, saturated colour blocks in pure channel values, monospace grid.
- Brand mapping [R]: client primary = the phosphor (all text tinted, the way a monochrome monitor is tinted); secondary = the cyan "header" colour; accent = the REC / intrusion colour; dark = the screen black (use a very dark tint of the brand rather than pure black); light = the bar white. On the colour-bars strip, map the brand colours into the bar positions and keep the remaining bars in true values so the strip still reads as a test pattern. If the brand is not amber/cyan-compatible, keep amber as "station default" and show brand colour as the channel tint.

## UI translation
- Buttons [R]: Fastext-style coloured blocks 1 row high with black label; the primary is a yellow or brand block with a leading `>`; press = invert colours.
- Tabs / segmented [R]: channel numbers `CH 01 02 03 04` in a row; active channel is white-on-colour; a tuning dial underneath (see below).
- Toggles [R]: channel selectors with `ON / OFF` words in brackets or a VHS-style slider with a notch; indicator lamp square.
- Inputs [R]: block cursor in a boxed field; label as teletext row with a coloured prefix cell; page-number entry `P___` with three underscores that fill.
- Cards / panels [R]: boxed teletext panels using mosaic border characters (solid colour edges 1 cell wide), a coloured title row, and a status line.
- Lists [R]: index pages with dotted leaders between title and page number (`WEATHER ............ 401`), alternate rows with different colour per category; right-aligned page numbers.
- Progress [R]: a tuning bar (frequency scale with ticks and a needle), a signal-strength meter (5 rising bars), or a mosaic loading bar; `SEARCHING...` text; `NO SIGNAL` state.
- Badges [R]: boxed words (`LIVE`, `REC`, `SUBS`, `HOLD`); inverse video.
- Dividers [R]: row of block characters; thin bars in a bar-colour sequence.
- Header / footer [R]: header is the teletext row 0 strip; footer is the Fastext bar; add a "ticker" line for the footer in a second row.
- Motion (static-safe) [R]: blinking cursor and tracking noise roll are optional; under reduced-motion show a single static tracking band and a steady `REC`.

1. "Test card": an original test pattern: seven brand-mapped bars, castellation blocks, greyscale ramp, grating lines and a station ident panel, framed by dashed safe-area lines and a rounded CRT mask.
2. "Page 100": a full 40 x 24 teletext page in monospaced cells with mosaic wordmark, headline double-height, cyan index list and a Fastext bar; one line corrupted with a cut-off and a block of noise.

## Pitfalls
- Scanline and glitch filters over a modern layout: the Local 58 critique says the aesthetic is only the stage, so the structure and copy must also behave like broadcast [S].
- Heavy RGB-split text: makes copy unreadable. Keep chroma split to 1-2px and body text clean.
- Fake Matrix green: not teletext. Use the broadcast palette.
- Copying Test Card F's child and clown or the BBC marks: copyright and trademark. Use an original diagnostic card.
- Mixing in pixel-art game iconography: becomes Pixel Arcade.
- Overdoing noise: legibility drops; keep noise to the borders.
- Real station or emergency-alert impersonation: use fictional idents.

## Distinctness vs existing aesthetics
- Retro-Futurist (70s console with LCD): a physical object with warm plastics. Pirate Broadcast: a screen and signal frame, teletext grid, timecode, chyrons.
- Pixel Arcade: game sprites and 8-bit icons. Pirate: broadcast structures (bars, pages, channels).
- ASCII Art: image made of characters. Pirate: a navigable page system with sixel mosaics and fixed colour roles.
- Cyber HUD: neon sci-fi interface. Pirate: analogue, degraded, diagnostic.
- Xerox: photocopy grit. Pirate: electronic scanlines and phosphor.

# 10. DYSTOPIAN LUXURY (the sinister fashion institution)

## The one insight
Severity here is made by removal and labelling, not by decoration. The 2017 Balenciaga site (Bureau Borsche with Demna) removed most interface: a small logo top-left, no imagery on the homepage, two products per row, product-only photos, small Arial text, black and white with a single neon-green add-to-bag button and a grey footer ([Its Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/news/bureau-borsche-balenciaga-website-redesign-010317); [Econsultancy review](https://www.econsultancy.com/luxury-ecommerce-review-is-balenciaga-s-normcore-website-more-than-a-gimmick/); [LS:N](https://lsnglobal.com/article/view/20931)) [S]. Margiela adds the second ingredient: a blank white label held by four stitches, then (1997) a circled-numeral system from 0 to 23 identifying product lines, categorical rather than chronological ([NSS Magazine](https://www.nssmag.com/en/fashion/19578/the-meaning-of-maison-martin-margiela-s-numbers); [Truss Archive](https://www.trussarchive.com/guides/how-to-identify-maison-margiela)) [S]. Museum accession numbers add the third: unique identifiers in the form year.sequence.part (2024.24.1), year first for sorting ([Collections Trust](https://collectionstrust.org.uk/resource/numbering/)) [S]. Put these together: stark neutral, one disruptive accent, and an institution that numbers and classifies everything. Lumon in Severance supplies the "benevolent institution" register.

## Signature mechanisms (concrete)

## Typography
- Class: default neutral grotesque (Arial/Helvetica register) for almost everything; a condensed bold or a high-contrast serif for rare display moments; extended sans or mono for labels.
- Google Fonts approximations [R]: Arial equivalent `Arimo`; grotesques `Inter Tight`, `Hanken Grotesk`, `Instrument Sans`, `Familjen Grotesk`, `Archivo`; condensed display `Anton` or `Bebas Neue` (Balenciaga's custom condensed is not on Google; "BB Condensed Bold" circulates but is unverified [S]); serif display `Bodoni Moda`, `Libre Caslon Display`, `Playfair Display` for the SSENSE-like masthead; extended `Michroma`, `Syncopate` for Lumon-like labels; mono for accession numbers `IBM Plex Mono`, `Courier Prime`.
- Case and size [R]: sentence case or all caps never mixed in the same block; body 12-13px (small on purpose but with 4.5:1 contrast; Econsultancy flagged small type as a usability cost [S]); giant display 160-400px; labels uppercase +8% tracking at 10px. Headlines built as one giant word, a small caption beside it, and a number.

## Colour: roles and brand mapping
- Fixed [R]: ink black (#0A0A0A), surgical white (#F7F7F5 or pure white), a mid grey for secondary text/footers (#8A8A8A; Balenciaga's footer is grey [S]), polished-metal greys for hardware, and exactly one red accent.
- Brand mapping [R]: client primary = the single accent. If primary is red, great; otherwise convert the brand primary into the accent only if it is saturated enough to be sharp on white and black, and keep blood red for danger/classified labels; if the brand is a dark colour, it becomes the "ink" (replacing black with a very dark brand tint). Secondary brand colour = tint in label strips (very small). Accent colour = the red. Light brand colour = the white (never replace white with a tinted pastel; the severity comes from near-pure neutrals).
- The ratio target is 92% neutrals, 7% metal/grey, 1-2% accent.

## UI translation
- Buttons [R]: rectangular, 0 radius, black fill with white uppercase 11px text or white with black 1px border; hover = invert; the one primary CTA per view uses the accent. No icons, no shadows.
- Tabs / segmented [R]: text links with a 1px underline for active; or a numbered row `01 02 03` in circled numerals. Segmented controls are hairline-boxed cells.
- Toggles [R]: a small square checkbox-like switch with an `X` mark for on, Margiela-stitch corners; or text pair `OFF / ON` with a strike-through on the inactive word.
- Inputs [R]: underline-only fields, labels in uppercase 10px, accession-style placeholders `0000.00.0`.
- Cards / panels [R]: no card chrome; a hairline top border, a number, a caption, a large image area (blank placeholder block in SVG, with crop marks).
- Lists [R]: ledger rows: accession number (mono) / item / status / date, separated by 1px lines, with a circled numeral column; a status like `ON LOAN`, `RESTRICTED`, `QUARANTINE`.
- Progress [R]: a thin 2px bar with a red end tick, percentage in huge numerals; or `STEP 3 OF 7` in label type.
- Badges [R]: security-label chips: black/white rectangles with a barcode or an `X`, a red-dot marker.
- Dividers [R]: 1px black line, double rule with registration tick marks, crop marks at corners.
- Header / footer [R]: tiny wordmark top-left, nav as three uppercase words, a counter (`CART (0)`); footer is a grey block with legal labels, an institutional address and a catalogue number [S, grey footer from Balenciaga].
- Motion (static-safe) [R]: cuts, not fades; image reveals by a hard mask wipe in one frame; reduced-motion = no wipe.

## Hero art ideas (SVG/CSS only)
1. "Accession": a gigantic cropped word in ink on white, a blank-label rectangle held by four stitch marks beside it with `2026.114.3` and a circled numeral, and one blood-red dot.
2. "Specimen": a vector garment or buckle detail (zip, strap, rivet) at huge scale, cropped to 40%, with a thin crop-mark frame and a classified-label strip across the lower edge.
3. "Corridor": a Lumon-like symmetrical white corridor in one-point perspective drawn with flat panels, a single green or brand-tint carpet strip, and a small red door tag.

## Pitfalls
- Gothic horror tropes (chains, crosses, skulls, gore): this is clinical, not goth.
- Red everywhere: blood red must be a single accent; more than 2% area loses the effect.
- Gradients, glows and glass: that becomes Aurora Noir or Liquid Glass.
- Serif-led warm magazine layouts: that becomes Editorial Serif. Keep the serif to a masthead only.
- Concrete textures and raw HTML styling: that is Raw Brutalist.
- Illegible tiny type: keep contrast high and respect minimum size; Econsultancy flagged Balenciaga's small type [S].
- Using real brand names, logos or Lumon marks: use fictional names and generic labels.
- Over-cropping so the board has no content: keep the controls row and list readable.

## Distinctness vs existing aesthetics
- Editorial Serif: warm literary serif and softness. Dystopian Luxury: neutral grotesque, clinical labels, extreme crop, one red accent.
- Aurora Noir: dark gradient glow. Dystopian Luxury: flat ink and white, hard cuts, metal only on hardware.
- Raw Brutalist: web-default and concrete. Dystopian Luxury: fashion-house severity, accession/security labels.
- Industrial Device / Industrial Hazard: functional machine signage. Dystopian Luxury: numbered institutional luxury; labels classify, they do not warn.

# Cross-aesthetic notes for the board builder

- Common board skeleton [R]: header strip, hero with signature art, palette row (with role names), type specimen (display, label, mono), controls row (buttons / tabs / toggles / inputs), dense list, footer. The signature mechanism must show up in each module, not only in the hero: stripes and lamps (Hazard), laminate patterns and primitives (Memphis), ribs and tubes (Biomech), grid and bars (Pirate), labels and crop (Luxury).
- Accessibility floor [R]: body text 4.5:1 minimum (watch amber on black, bone on charcoal, small grey on white); motion off under `prefers-reduced-motion`; state never conveyed by colour alone (add words/glyphs).
- Brand colour behaviour summary [R]:
  - Hazard: brand = machine enamel; hazard ladder fixed.
  - Memphis: brand = dominant flat plus pattern accent; extra borrowed hues allowed.
  - Biomech: brand = dim bioluminescent glow in a monochrome material.
  - Pirate: brand = phosphor tint plus bar colours; amber/cyan are fallbacks.
  - Luxury: brand = the single accent or the ink; neutrals fixed.
- Google Fonts quick list by aesthetic [R]: Hazard (Big Shoulders Display, Barlow Condensed, IBM Plex Mono, B612 Mono); Memphis (Unbounded, Bagel Fat One, Jost, DM Mono); Biomech (Cormorant, Cinzel, Michroma, Xanh Mono); Pirate (DotGothic16, Silkscreen, Oswald, Share Tech Mono); Luxury (Arimo, Inter Tight, Anton, Bodoni Moda, IBM Plex Mono).
- Differences between the three "machine" boards [R]: Hazard = heavy, grey, state and permission; Pirate = light on dark, grid, signal and time; Biomech = organic, monochrome, rhythm and growth.
